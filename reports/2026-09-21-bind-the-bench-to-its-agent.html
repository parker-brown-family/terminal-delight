Bind the bench to its agent

A bench belongs to the conversation that made it, not to the square of screen it was drawn in. This is the shape of the half you kept: the store, the key, the sentinel, and the rule that stops a surface being lost between two writes. Nothing is coded. Does figure 05 match what you expect a /clear to do?

My reading: a clear begins a new conversation and its bench opens empty; a compaction carries the same one on and keeps everything. I had this inverted for most of today, so it is the one drawing I would most like you to look at twice.

Recommend yes. It buys the case in figure 04 and spends forgery‑resistance: every agent runs as you, and every ledger entry is writable by all of them. The threat measured is two honest agents confused by one shell, not one attacking the other. inferred

Method and limits

what is not established

Nothing here was built

No code was written for this page. Every signature is a proposal and every test is a name. What has been built is the other pane’s ledger reader, on its own branch, uncommitted.

Unmeasured, and marked as such

  • Whether /clear mints a new session id. Zero genuine /clear invocations in the 600 most recent transcripts on this machine, so nothing in the history can settle it. The seq segment is correct under either answer, which is why it can be built before the measurement lands.
  • What a long conversation actually accumulates. The 64 cap is untested against a real week.
  • The turn ordinal is counted by the window watching the terminal, so a latch it misses shifts every later number. No harness‑supplied ordinal exists at this boundary.

Where the isolation stops

The mailbox stays writable by any process running as you — that is what makes file drops work. What changes is that a stranger’s file can no longer become a conversation’s record. And the ledger’s integrity rests on filesystem ownership rather than on anything cryptographic; a forged root was fixed at both ends after it was found propagating into later legitimate entries.
