Lookup

You find a pane in this window by eye. The sticky note comes first, then the pane's title, then which tab it sits in under which branch of the left bar. Each of those is a name, and none of them could be typed. Ctrl+Shift+F searches what panes have printed. Nothing searched what they are called. Alt+L does that now, in find's own frame, and Enter takes you there.

What proves it

F1 lists Alt+L beside Alt+A in all nine languages, and the docs site's keys page names it in its three places.

Three choices you might overrule

1 · Should a pane also be found by its working directory?

You asked for titles, and titles are what it matches. Adding the directory would let alt-l-lookup find the pane sitting in that worktree. It would also make terminal-delight light every pane in the repository, which buries the one you wanted.

Recommendation: leave it to titles, notes and the bar's names. Add the directory as its own row kind if you find yourself wanting it.

2 · Read the names once, when it opens?

The panel takes a snapshot when Alt+L is pressed. An agent that renames its pane while the panel is up shows the old title until you reopen it. Enter still finds the right pane, because the jump goes by the pane's identity and not its name.

Recommendation: keep the snapshot. The panel is up for seconds, and a list that reorders under your fingers is worse than one that is a second stale.

3 · Alt+L costs the shell one key

In bash and zsh, Alt+L lower-cases the next word (readline's downcase-word). The window now takes the chord, as it already takes Alt+R, Alt+W, Alt+V and Alt+H.

Recommendation: accept it. Nothing in this window's composer used it.
