Squaring the new-tab circle

Your two habits are the same verb pointed at different targets, and only the target was ever ambiguous. new_tab already does the structurally right thing — it reads place_of(self.active) and lands the tab at its branch's end, with a comment explaining that a tab landing loose would open somewhere neither the strip nor the tree is pointing. Nothing about that needs changing. What needs changing is that neither entry point says where it is aiming.

The tree row aims at itself

A + fades in on a project or group row under the cursor, and New tab here leads that row's right-click menu. Target is the row you are pointing at, whatever is active, with no selection step and no chord. This is the gesture you said you expected, and it is the discoverable one.

The header button says its target out loud

Ctrl+shift+T and the strip's + keep meaning “a tab in the branch I am working in”. The difference is that the button grows a caption — + tab in UX — so the answer is on screen before you press it rather than after.

Same glyph, same size, a few hundred pixels apart, and nothing on either says which. Relabelling the bar's to +▮ with a tooltip, or folding it into the header tray below, separates them.

Both paths call one new_tab, both land at branch_end. The tree row is for pointing; the chord is for muscle memory. Neither is “the” way.

The thing that will bite

A drop target you can reach by accident, whose payload is running agents, sitting at the bottom of a scrolling list. The mitigation that costs nothing: the can only accepts a drag that has engaged (the bar's drag system already distinguishes armed from engaged), and a delete that took more than one pane shows a four-second undo strip at the foot of the bar before the tray is the only way back.

What a deleted group should mean

You said FULLY recover, which rules out re-creating terminals at the same paths and calling it recovery. An agent mid-turn does not come back that way. These are the three shapes that deliver, or fail to deliver, what you asked for.

chosen · built

A · Hold in the window

Delete moves the Tab values into a held list with a deadline instead of dropping them. Recover puts the group back with its tabs, their order, their names and their project.

measured hangup returns immediately when self.attach is None, which is the window-owned mode you are running today — so a close does not kill the shell by asking, it kills it by dropping the last reference to the pane. Keeping the value is the mechanism here, and the same change reads as “skip hangup” once the host owns the pseudoterminals.

The only one that delivers full recovery with the code as it stands, and it reuses the numbers already approved: four hours for a group, one for a lone tab, ten holdings at a cap.

What it costs you: a held group dies when the window does. Quitting Terminal Delight ends every held shell, which is the opposite of what the approved spec promises, so the tray has to say “while this window is open” and mean it.

B · Tombstone the layout

Record the group's shape — tabs, names, working directories, order, which panes were agents — kill the shells immediately, and respawn from that record on recover, with claude --resume where it applies.

Genuinely better at one thing: it is data in the layout file, so it survives a relaunch and a crash, which A does not.

What it costs you: it re-creates rather than recovers. Scrollback gone, a running turn gone, anything unsaved gone. It answers a smaller question than the one you asked.

C · Wait for the flip

Build the menus and the trash now with delete disabled, and turn it on when the host owns the pseudoterminals, at which point a held pane survives the window dying too.

One build instead of two, and the promise on the tray is the true one from the first day it appears.

What it costs you: “how do I delete a group” stays unanswered until the flip lands, and slice 1 of that work is still open. You asked today.

How the window got parked

docs/plans/client-server/00-status.md

9 September — the amendment

A fresh-agent review found that a final close never reaches its save: the process quits while the explicit-shrink flag and the save sit below an early return, so the host keeps the pre-close layout and the next launch replans the closed leaf as a respawn.

Your reading of that was not that the save should move up, but that an immediate durable delete was the wrong promise in the first place — someone who closes the last pane of a tab may simply have missed. The commit that records it is titled “Closing a pane now starts a clock instead of ending a process” and moves no code.

What it pinned down, in its own words:

  • One pane held for an hour, two or more held for four, counted in panes rather than in the verb that closed them — which is why a group delete inherits four hours without anyone deciding again.
  • Retention needs a cap as well as a timer, or ordinary churn holds a hundred process groups. Ten most recent.
  • The live process and the layout entry are separate retentions that die at different times, and reopening has to say which one you got.
  • Ctrl+shift+z, not ctrl+shift+t, because that chord is already this terminal's new-tab binding and every other Linux terminal's.
  • Closing the app or the window expires nothing, which was already the point of the feature.

10 September — the re-cut

That section of 01-product.md describes the iteration after the flip, not the build that flips. Close keeps today's immediate meaning until the held state and the reopen key land, which is how the meaning still ships exactly once — the reason the amendment was ordered before the flip in the first place.

Which is a good decision and the reason the promise is not in the binary. It also means the last bullet above — surviving a window close — is the one thing option A cannot buy you, because it needs the host to be the one holding the pseudoterminal.

What is true in the binary right now

  • measured No ctrl+shift+z binding.
  • measured No held-tab or tombstone store. The thirteen matches for “held” and “tombstone” in main.rs are prose, a geometry variable, and the graveyard that scans disk for dead agent sessions — a different feature that recovers a conversation, not a process.
  • measured The confirm dialog says, in as many words, “This can't be undone.” It is telling the truth.
