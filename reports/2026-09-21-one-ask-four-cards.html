What you photographed

Where the second card comes from

Three subsystems can each turn an AskUserQuestion into a card. They were built at different times for different reasons and all three are still wired. Here they are one at a time; the boxes never move.

Why it asked you one at a time

This is the second, separate defect, and it is the one you asked about directly. The batch behaviour you want already exists. It is on one of three roads, and that road was closed before you pressed anything.

The design fault, stated once. Whether the bench can answer a multi-part question as one submission currently depends on whether you happen to be looking at the bench at that moment. Glance at the terminal to check something and the interaction silently degrades to one-question-at-a-time, with no message, for the rest of the round.

What it should do instead

Decisions

Three, and the third is only a sequencing call.

1 · How does one question get one identity?

ShapeWhat it changesWhat it leaves
A — one id function derive.rs mints ask-hook-<tuid>-<i> like the channel does, from the tool-use id it already reads. The screen race and the picked.is_none() guard.
B — A, plus close both directions recommended Also: drop the picked.is_none() clause so an answered question still matches its own card, and retire any ask-live- twin when a hook round arrives naming the same text. Three producers still exist; nothing structurally prevents a fourth.
C — one owner The channel becomes the only thing that presents a question surface. The screen reader and the transcript reader feed it facts — a cursor, an answer — and mint nothing. Nothing. This is the shape where the bug class cannot recur.

B now, C as the direction. B is small, testable and fixes what you saw. C is the honest answer and is a plan of its own — it re-keys what a question surface is, which is the same weight as the conversation re-keying that got four gates.

2 · How do the three answers get submitted together?

ShapeWhat it doesRisk
Keep the hold open recommended, first The window keeps the marker fresh on a cadence regardless of which face is showing, and the face alone decides whether the hook may hold — instead of the clock going stale the moment you look away. Low, but it needs care: the staleness rule is what stops a dead window holding an agent hostage for nine minutes. A live window on the TERM face is not a dead window; the rule currently cannot tell them apart.
Move the gate above the road recommended, second A press always records. A round only leaves the bench when it is complete, on whatever road is available, behind an explicit SUBMIT ROUND. Real. The arrow-key road has to walk the picker through three answers, re-reading the screen between each. Worth a tracer slice before committing to it.
Say so instead Leave the roads alone; draw on the card that this round is being answered one at a time and why. None, and it does not give you what you asked for. Listed because it is the cheap honest fallback if the second shape turns out fragile.

The first removes the reason the bad road was ever taken; the second makes the bad road behave when it must be taken. They are independent and the first is much cheaper.

3 · What ships first?

Two things on this list need no decision and are not code: install the branch build (the stacked-picker fix is sitting in 9499efe unshipped), and re-run the hook installer so the pane is on TDAC 0.2 — without which the next diagnosis of this has the same blind spot: a released line with no clock and one word covering two different causes.

Suggested order: install both, then B, then keep-the-hold-open, then the gate move behind a tracer slice.
