Marks, Not Words

terminal-delight · a UX pass over every shipped palette · 18 September 2026

Three separate passes went over this chrome today and each of them found the same defect wearing a different role. Mine was ink_off, which is Faint, painting the word on every tab in the window. The card pass found th.faint doing it at forty-two sites — subtitles, cost lines, provenance. This pass found th.complement doing it at thirteen more, including the words PLUGINS and NOTIFICATIONS and the sentence a search shows when nothing matches.

Every ink, every palette

The pattern

The rule is already written down. It is in the ink table's own // ---- text ---- heading, which groups five inks and implies the rest are not for words. It is in emphasis::meta, whose doc ends "Faint keeps its job: marks, not words". It is in the tab fix from this morning. Three statements of the same rule, in three files, and not one of them is read at the moment somebody types text_color(th.faint).

There is a fourth, and it is the sharpest. pane.rs floors the complement's lightness to 0.5–0.92 before painting the pane title with it, and says why in a comment: "so a dark complement override stays legible on the header". One call site found the whole problem, solved it, and kept the solution to itself — the other thirteen paint in the raw role.

Why the gate is not built yet

I went to write it and stopped at the first candidate. The tab strip's close affordance — the × you click to shut a tab — is painted th.faint when the tab is not the active one, which measures the same 1.2:1 as everything else in figure 1. But it is a glyph, not a word, and the rule as written says Faint is for marks. A blanket ban on the role inside text_color would fail on that line, and on the chevrons and ticks beside it, and a check that cries wolf gets switched off — after which nothing is enforced at all.

So the gate needs one decision first, and it is yours rather than mine: is a clickable glyph a mark or a word? An × you are meant to find and press is not a divider, and 1.2:1 is the number at which a person hunts for it. If it is a control, the rule is "anything a person reads or presses takes a voice" and the gate is a straight ban. If it stays a mark, the gate needs an explicit opt-out marker at each such site, which is more ceremony but keeps the exceptions in one greppable list instead of scattered through main.rs.

Where the palettes actually differ

That is the second pattern, and it is the one to take next. Every state and marking colour in the chrome is calibrated on a dark ground — the ok and warn inks are the bright ANSI slots, chosen because they read against near-black. On quiet-command, the one light palette we ship, select measures 1.39:1, ok 2.02:1 and warn 1.90:1 against the panel they sit on. The label is legible and the signal is not.

What I expected to find and did not

The chrome promises three meaning-hues that are three different claims — the accent is furniture, select is you, live is something running — and when a theme declares none of them, complement and human are both derived from accent.h + 0.5. That looked like a collision waiting to happen, and four of the six themes declare neither. It is not one: in CIE Lab the closest pair on any palette is live against warn on quiet-command at ΔE 15.6, and everything else is 29 or above, where about 2.3 is the just-noticeable difference. The saturation and lightness the derivation applies are doing the work the hue does not.

Worth saying because the instrument nearly lied about it. Contrast ratio put mark and select at 1.10:1 on hacker, which reads as identical — but contrast is luminance only, and the two are 107 ΔE apart. Two colours can match in brightness and share nothing else.

Three small things

  • mark_dim has no callers at all, and ink_ghost has two. Both fail the floor on every palette, which costs nothing while nobody uses them and is a loaded gun in a table people copy recipes out of.
  • select is the only ink in the table that is a marking colour but reads like a text colour — it is the one the tab fix now uses for a border and a seat and never for a word. Moving it out of the text neighbourhood in the file would make that structural rather than remembered.
  • The palettes — tide, retro, last horizon and the other eight under ~/.local/share/terminal-delight/palettes/ — are still unmeasured. They arrive through the paint/dynamic path, so skin --theme cannot reach them and no in-repo test can gate them. That is a hole in this pass, not a clean result.
