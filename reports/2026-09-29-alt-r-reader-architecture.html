You concurred with all five on 29 September, so nothing here is waiting on you. Program design and the slices follow as one plan.

1 · Join a shell’s lines on the terminal’s flag, with no copy of the grid?

Exact on both cores and cheaper than re-wrapping a 16–58 MB copy; the joined history is cached and the terminal is no longer locked for the whole read.

Recommendation: yes. rio-vt’s re-wrap stays unused, and its count of rows gone off the top keeps the cache cheap.

2 · Draw a conversation in the reader’s own text layout?

It keeps fast scrolling through huge transcripts, selection and copy. Headings are bold rather than larger, and tables are aligned text. The Markdown document view draws real tables but builds every block every frame and has no selection.

Recommendation: the reader’s own layout, as figure 04 of Gate 1 drew it.

3 · Show a reply when each block of it is finished?

Claude Code writes a block to the transcript only when it’s done. Until then the words exist only on screen, so the live footer carries them.

Recommendation: yes, with the footer showing the pane’s rows from the input box up.

4 · Keep what figure 07 keeps?

Prompts and replies shown, one line per tool call with its output behind a click, thinking hidden, the rest skipped.

Recommendation: as drawn; note any row you want otherwise.

5 · Lend documents, and let a small picture stay at its own pixels?

Lending keeps notes, links and the saved layout untouched. Fitting a small picture to the glass would enlarge it into blur; the zoom keys still go larger.

Recommendation: lend, and keep a picture at no more than its own pixels until you zoom.

What this page does not settle

what was read rather than run, and what nobody has looked at yet

  • Nothing was built or run. Every behaviour on this page is read from the code, and the timings come from the code’s own comments. inferred
  • That a reply appears only when each block finishes comes from the records on disk; it has not been timed against a live turn.
  • The rebuild-every-frame cost of today’s reader follows from how the content generation and the cache key work; nobody has measured it.
  • The app counts a binding made by launch time as certain, and it picks the closest of agents launched close together. After a /clear, before the new file exists, a pane can be bound to its own previous conversation. It can’t be bound to another pane’s.
  • No /clear rotation has been observed in the session ledger yet, so following one is designed, not seen.
  • Codex writes a different format and needs its own reader of it: a medium job, left to a later slice.
  • Video decoding at the reader’s size has an unmeasured CPU cost.
  • A pane attached while vim was up has no history until vim exits; the reader should say so rather than show an empty page.
