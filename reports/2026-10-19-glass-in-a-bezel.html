Glass in a bezel

Terminal Delight · CRT pane edges · 2026-10-19 · branch feat/glass-rim

You pointed at a set of mock-up monitors and said the corners were the thing: each screen sits inside its frame, its glass rounded at the corners, with a soft shadow where the frame's lip overhangs the glass, darkest right where two edges meet.

What a TD pane was doing instead

A bent pane in TD gets its curve from a pixel pass on the GPU (the CRT pass). It pushes the picture outward with a barrel formula and paints black wherever the formula lands off the edge of the screen. The edge was therefore a hard cut into black, and each corner came to a point, because a barrel-bent rectangle still has square corners, just tilted. Figure 2 shows what changes when those corners are rounded.

Where the rounding has to happen

The first idea, drawing a rounded frame over the pane in ordinary UI, does not work on a bent pane, because the pass bends everything inside the screen, a frame drawn there included. So the rounding went into the pass. It decides which pixels are glass by asking where each one comes from in the unbent picture, and accepts only points inside a rounded rectangle. The warp then curves the rounded corners along with the text, and the two always agree. Rounding alone would still look cut out of the frame, though, rather than set into it.

Three more things happen at the edge of the glass. The frame's shadow falls across it, heavier under the top edge than above the bottom one, as if lit from above. That shadow is deepened again near each corner, where two edges' shadows overlap. And outside the glass, a faint lit line runs along the frame's edge, brighter along the bottom, which faces the light.

The moulding round the glass

The glass now sits in a recess, but the frame around it was still plain black. In your reference the frame is a different colour from the glass, and its inner edge slopes down into the recess. Where that slope turns a corner, its shading turns with it. So the bent pane now has a faint moulding around the glass. It is a thin ring in the pane's own chrome colour, drained of most of its saturation so it reads as plastic rather than as more screen. The pass shades it as a slope lit from above. The slopes that face up toward the light, along the bottom and right, catch a little of it. The ones that face down, along the top and left, fall into shade. Around each corner the shading runs smoothly from one to the other, and that is what gives the inside of the curve its shape.

Your worry was the warp slider: the space around the glass grows and shrinks with it. The ring therefore fades out before it can reach the tube's straight outer boundary, so no setting can turn it into a hard rectangular frame. At low warp only the corners have room for it, which is where you wanted it.

Flat panes, and what is yours to argue with

A pane with warp at zero skips the pass entirely, so it gets the same seat drawn the ordinary way. Its square corners are filled with the pane's own background, and soft shadows are laid inside a rounded edge. It applies to any theme with a vignette, except paper skins. On a dark theme it is quiet, because the screen and the frame are nearly the same black.

Yellow = numbers I picked by eye, open to change.
  • Corner radius: 5.5% of the screen's short side, held between 10 and 72 device pixels. Your reference is about the same proportion.
  • Shadow depth: 4.5% of the short side. At the very edge it takes out about 94% of the light under the top lip and about 72% above the bottom one.
  • Moulding: the pane's chrome hue at no more than 22% saturation and 30% lightness, at 40% strength. The ring is 2.8% of the short side wide, held between 8 and 34 device pixels.
  • There is no slider. The edge and the moulding are always on for a bent pane, on every face, bench and documents included. The strength already travels to the pass per pane, so a GAUGES dial would be grade-channel work only, and I have left it out until you ask for one.
  • A flat pane gets the rounded edge but no moulding.

How it travels

The pass lives in a patched copy of Zed's renderer, so the change is one more patch in that stack, docs/patches/0008-glass-rim.patch, applied by scripts/prepare-gpui.sh. It adds one renderer function beside the existing one rather than changing it. The bezel's colour and strength ride two slots each tube already had and never used, so no other branch's build breaks. A new test caught a bug on the way: the colour travels as a number, and the shader's first reading of it rounded some colours up a step. Pure white would have come back as black. The patch was built against a private copy of the renderer checkout, because the shared one on this machine already carries another agent's unmerged renderer experiment.
