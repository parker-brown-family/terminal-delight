The left bar's bottom slot

The empty space under the tree becomes a slot: a stack of small widgets the bar draws and the layout file remembers. It ships with two — a wrapped session/weekly allowance bar, and the agent-state rollup shrunk to one line — and the right-click menu that swaps skin, inverts the reading, and tabs between providers.

2026-09-11 · ~/Work/td-left-bar branch left-bar · mockups are live HTML, sampled from the bar's own palette · reading budget ~10 min

Read first

  1. 1

    A missing allowance must never be drawn as an empty bar

    This widget is the exact shape that breaks the unknown-is-not-zero rule: a provider with no collector installed, an expired sign-in, or a vendor that publishes no weekly window all produce no reading — and a bar's natural default for no reading is a bar of length zero, which reads as "you are out". Every variant below carries a hatched unknown state, and it is the state to look at hardest.

  2. 2

    Pick the variant — four are drawn, one ships

    Four readings of "outer bar is the session, inner ticks are the week", from the literal sci-fi shell to a two-hairline version that looks like the rest of the bar. They differ in how much they survive being 240px wide and 15px tall, which is the only width that matters.

  3. 3

    Providers are already data, not code

    usage.rs keys every subscription by a record id and says so in its own header: publish a record under a new id and the panel gains a tab. The provider tab strip costs a logo and a row — Anthropic and OpenAI today, anything with a collector tomorrow, with no change to this widget.

  4. 4

    REMAINING is a render-time flip, never a stored number

    Limit.percent is the fraction used, as the vendor published it. The invert toggle computes 1.0 − percent at paint. Storing the inverted value would mean a config change silently rewrites what a record says.

  5. 5

    One skin now, but the registry ships with it

    A skin chooses shape; the theme still chooses colour. That split is what stops one skin becoming a hundred and sixty-five theme rules, and it is why a second skin later is a new file rather than a new palette.

the colour rule all four are drawn under

Geometry tells the two windows apart; colour tells calm from not. The worse of the two sets the status colour and both readings wear it — which is why every Claude row above is red end to end on a session that is 91% free. Letting each window carry its own colour was tried first and produced a green bar with one red cell, which reads as a healthy bar with a decoration on it.

The corollary, and the reason variant B's lit ticks have a white rim: a reading must never depend on hue alone, or a red tick on a red wash is two things the same colour.

the failure this widget is shaped to produce

A bar with no reading and a bar reading zero are one pixel apart, and the wrong one is the confident one. Limit has percent: f32 with a Default — anything that reaches for unwrap_or_default() on a missing window draws a full "you are out of tokens" bar from a record that never said anything.

measuredThe record type already models absence correctly: limits is a Vec<Limit>, so a vendor that publishes no weekly window contributes no entry rather than a zeroed one, and has_prompt_stats exists precisely so a billing-API collector "drops those two numbers rather than printing a confident zero" (usage.rs:96). The widget has to keep that distinction rather than re-collapse it at paint.

inferredStale is a third thing again: the number was true, and is no longer current. It dims and carries the age instead of disappearing, because a four-hour-old session reading is still worth more than nothing.

Five groups, and four of them are the ones asked for. Provider is a tab strip with each vendor's own mark, grouped in a single inset well so it reads as one control rather than two buttons. Reading is the invert, titled REMAINING and on by default. Skin ships one chip and two dashed slots — the registry is visible from day one so the second skin is obviously a file, not a rewrite.

Windows is the group the record forces: Claude Code publishes three limits today — Session, Weekly, and a Fable Weekly — so "the inner ticks are the weekly" is a default, not a shape. The widget shows two at a time and this is where you say which two.

Slot is the flexible half of the ask, in its plainest form: the slot holds a list of widgets, and this is the list. Turning both off leaves the tree the whole column back.

Yours to decide

Which variant ships?

A shell, B inset, C two-tier, D notched. They are all drawn above at the width they will actually live at, so the comparison is real rather than a description.

B · Inset rail. It holds both readings at 15px without inventing a silhouette the bar does not otherwise use, and the leading-edge treatment means the session can be a wash of colour while the ticks stay the bright thing. A is the better screenshot and the worse instrument.

Is the slot a stack, or one widget with a picker?

You asked for both widgets at once, which already answers it for today. The question is whether the architecture says "the slot holds a list" or "the slot holds a widget and you choose which".

A list. A list degenerates to one widget for free; a picker cannot grow to two without a rewrite. The cost is one height budget to enforce, so the tree is never squeezed by a stack somebody forgot about.

When the session and the week disagree, which one colours the bar?

The demo case is the live one: 91% of the session left, 13% of the week. Green outer bar with one red tick, or the whole thing red?

Worst-of drives the colour, both keep their own geometry. Record::worst_limit() already exists and is the vendor-agnostic answer. A bar that looks calm while the week is nearly gone is a bar that lies once a week.

A provider with no collector — dead tab, or no tab?

Today OpenAI's tab exists only if something writes a Codex record. If nothing does, the tab strip silently has one entry and the feature looks broken rather than unconfigured.

Show the tab, dead, carrying its own fix. Record already has status_text and auth_help ("Run claude auth login…") for exactly this, and an absent tab teaches nothing. This is the unknown-is-not-zero rule applied to the tab strip rather than to the bar.

Does right-click on the widget steal the bar's context menu?

The bar's rows already answer to right-click for rename and config. A widget that opens its own menu is a second meaning for the same gesture inside one column.

Yes, inside the slot's own bounds, and nowhere else. The slot is a distinct region with a visible top edge; the rows keep their menu, the widgets get theirs. Worth naming now because it is the sort of thing that is discovered by a person confused about which menu they are looking at.

The stopwatch and the calendar

glyph choice

Why two glyphs and not a legend

The widget is fifteen pixels tall in a column two hundred and sixty wide. There is no room for the words "Session (5-hour)" and "Weekly (7-day)", which is what the usage panel has room for and prints. A stopwatch is the shortest possible spelling of "this window is hours long"; a calendar is the shortest possible spelling of "this one is days long".

They are also the pair a person can tell apart at 11px, which rules out most of the alternatives: an hourglass and a calendar are two rectangles, a clock and a calendar are a circle and a rectangle but "clock" reads as wall-clock time rather than an elapsed window.

Where each one sits

The stopwatch caps the outer bar, because the outer bar is the session. The calendar belongs to the tick rail — in variants A, B and D it rides the rail's left end inside the shell; in C it sits under the stopwatch, one per tier.

A widget drawing only one window drops the other glyph rather than greying it. A greyed glyph is an assertion that the window exists and is empty, which is the mistake this whole document keeps circling.

The label the vendor gave

Limit.label carries the vendor's own words. The glyph is a shorthand for it, not a replacement — the hover and the right-click menu both spell the label out, so the shorthand is never the only place the window is named.

Narrow

the bar is resizable, and people drag it small

The bar is a drag handle away from half this width

left_bar_w is user-set and clamped between LEFT_BAR_MIN and LEFT_BAR_MAX, with a hairline resize handle on the right edge. The mockups draw 260px because that is the width the state-file test uses, but the widget has to survive the minimum as well as the default.

What degrades, in order

  • The trailing percentage goes first. It is a convenience; the bar itself is the reading.
  • Then the tick count drops from seven to five to three. A tick narrower than two pixels is a texture, not a count — better to quantise coarser than to draw a grey mush.
  • The glyphs stay to the end. Losing them makes the bar anonymous, and an anonymous bar in a sidebar is worse than no bar.

The counter

Variant A holds its width naturally — five fixed cells of tabular digits. Variant B reflows, which is the second argument against it after the moving-target one.

Vendor marks

what a provider tab actually draws

The tabs above are drawn, not copied

The two marks in the popover mockup are hand-built SVG approximations — an eight-spoke asterisk and a pair of interlocking hexagons — good enough to show the layout and not good enough to ship. Shipping the real marks is a licensing question rather than a drawing one, and it is the sort of question that is cheap now and expensive after a release.

Three ways out, in preference order

  • The vendor's own brand asset under its brand guidelines. Both companies publish marks for exactly this use — identifying their product in an integration. This is the normal path and almost certainly the answer.
  • A glyph the collector publishes. The record could carry its own icon field, the way a theme carries icon: String. This pushes the question to whoever writes the collector, which is where it belongs for a third-party provider TD has never heard of.
  • Initials in the provider's colour. The fallback, and what an unknown provider gets regardless — a tab with no mark still needs to be a tab.

The third one has to exist whatever happens to the first two, because the tab strip is open-ended by construction.

The slot contract

what a widget must promise the bar

Four promises

  • A declared height. The slot sums them and the tree keeps the rest. A widget that sizes itself from its content can push the tree out of the column on a busy day, which is a bug that only appears when you are busiest.
  • No colour of its own. Everything comes out of the theme — accent, faint, text. A skin chooses shape. This is the line that keeps one skin from turning into a hundred and sixty-five theme rules, which is a mistake this house has made once already and written down.
  • Paint from a snapshot. The bar rebuilds every frame and clears its drop-target registry as it goes. A widget that reads a file or waits on anything during paint is a stutter in a surface that is on screen constantly.
  • An unknown state that is not a zero state. Non-negotiable, and the one thing worth a test per widget rather than a review.

What a widget gets

  • The width it has been given, which changes under the drag handle.
  • The scale factor, so it matches the rest of the chrome at every grade.
  • Its own preferences blob out of the layout file, and a right-click within its bounds.

What it does not get

Any way to drive anything. The wall is a read-only scoreboard on purpose and the slot inherits that: a widget that could act would make a sidebar into a control surface, and every glance would carry a risk of a click.

How to prove this wrong

the checks to run before building, and after

If any of these holds, the widget is the wrong answer

  • Nobody can read two windows in one bar. Put variant B on the bar, spend a day, and answer "how much of the week is left" without hovering. If the answer needs a hover, the wrapping is decoration and variant C — or two separate rows — is what to build.
  • The records are usually stale. If updated_at is routinely older than the session window, the bar is a museum piece and the work belongs in the collector, not the widget. Check before building: read the state directory and look at the ages.
  • There is only ever one provider. If no Codex record ever appears on this machine, the tab strip is ceremony around a single value and the provider control should be a line of text instead.
  • The slot loses to the tree. If the bar is routinely scrolled, 44px of widget is 44px of tree, and the slot should be collapsible by default rather than on.

The one that would show up as a defect

A provider with no weekly window drawing a full or empty weekly rail. That is the invalidation criterion worth a test rather than an opinion: construct a Record with one Limit, paint it, and assert the second rail is in its unknown state. It goes red before the fix exists, or the test is not testing anything.
