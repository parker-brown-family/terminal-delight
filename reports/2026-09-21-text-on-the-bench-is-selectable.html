Dragging over the bench selects its text

The workbench draws text nobody can take. A comment summary on the rail, a reply on a card, the line you typed into the composer — all of it is pixels, and the only way to get a sentence out of it today is to retype it. This puts a real selection over the regions that carry reading — the card body and the rail — and leaves the buttons alone. Two things changed since the first draft of this page, and both are yours to settle: the composer is no longer in scope by default, because the mirror it is made of is being replaced, and a press on the card body or a rail row will act when you let go rather than when you push down.

difficulty 6 / 10

Read first

  1. Presses on the card body and rail rows move to mouse-up

    They fire on mouse-down today. A drag has to start somewhere, and both of those places are already zones — so the press has to wait to find out whether it was a click or the beginning of a drag. Buttons, tabs and dials keep firing on push.

    4–6px slop threshold · affects Hit::Arm and Hit::OpenRow only

  2. The composer fell out of scope while this page was being written

    You asked for it by name. Since then a sibling pane got your ruling that the workbench is being decoupled from the terminal view, and that no more repairs go into the mirror. The composer is the mirror, and the one design decision I made for it — read-only, because it cannot type over a range the far end does not know about — is a constraint the decouple removes. So it is drawn as out, and question one asks whether you want it back.

    the other two regions are unaffected either way

  3. Scope is decided by geometry, not by call site

    Every text run on the bench registers itself. Which runs are selectable is then a rectangle test against the measured regions — the card body and the rail. Nothing has to be threaded through the twenty-odd drawing functions, and your "not the menus" line is drawn on the surface rather than maintained by hand in a list.

    two probe calls, the same element dial_drop already uses

  4. The highlight lags the atom list by one frame, on purpose

    A run's position is only known once it has been laid out, so the newest measurement any frame can draw with is the one the frame before it recorded. The dial menu is placed by exactly this trick already and it is exact rather than approximate, for the same reason.

    no new machinery — the atom list is read, then cleared, at the top of the build

  5. A selection that silently drops a line looks identical to one that works

    This is the failure mode worth spending the tests on. The selection model is pure functions over a list of rectangles and strings, so the ordering, the partial ends and the line breaks in the copied text are all table-testable without rendering anything.

    the invalidation check: drag the whole card, copy, diff against the surface JSON

What the gesture does

Where it lands in what already exists

The bench already has half of this built, for a different reason. Because the tube bends the picture and gpui hit-tests the flat tree, no bench element carries a click handler of its own: every control records its flat rectangle as it paints, and the pane un-bends the pointer and looks it up. A selection needs the same list with a different payload.

What lands on the clipboard

A browser gets line breaks for free because it knows which elements are blocks. Here every run is a sibling in a flex tree and nothing says whether two of them were on the same line. The join rule is therefore geometric, and a mistake in it would be hard to see: the highlight on screen stays perfect while the text on the clipboard runs together.

That check fails today on any join rule that drops a run, which is the failure this whole table exists to prevent — a selection that looks right and copies eleven of twelve lines.

Slices

Yours to decide

You asked for the composer. Do you still want it, knowing the mirror is going?

Your words were "including the text entry user text box". Since then a sibling pane got your ruling on the decouple — "no more fixes to the mirror", the fifteen small repairs cancelled because they would be thrown away. The composer is the mirror. Its selection would be read-only because it is a mirror, and that is the constraint the rework removes; a real editor wants a selection you can type over, which is a different feature.

Recommend: leave it out, and take it in the rework. The other two regions carry what you actually reached for — a comment summary and a card. If you want it anyway it is roughly a day, its TextLayout already exists, and I will say on the page that it is scaffolding.

Presses on the card body and rail rows act on release. Accept?

A drag has to start on something, and those two places are the two Parker named. The alternative keeps them acting on push and only lets a drag begin on bare space between paragraphs — which, on a densely packed card, is almost nowhere.

Recommend: accept the deferral. Five pixels of slop, and a click that does not move still feels immediate. Every native list on this desk works this way.

Verb chips inside a card: highlightable, or holes in the selection?

They sit inside the body rectangle, so a drag passing over comment or ask agent would highlight the word and copy it. A browser does exactly this with button labels. Excluding them means a second geometry test and a gap in the middle of a copied paragraph.

Recommend: let them highlight. The chip labels are one word each, and a hole in a selection is stranger than an extra word.

Anything else on the bench you want inside the fence?

The two regions are the card body and the rail. The agent strip, shelf tabs, gallery chrome and pane header are out by your line. The conversation tail and the empty-bench copy are already in, by sitting inside the body region.

Recommend: ship these two and add a third only when something turns out to be unreachable.

Method and limits

Everything above is read out of the source on origin/main — re-read there after the first draft turned out to have been written against a checkout 66 commits behind it — and not run. measured covers what the code says: window_chord's refusal to take ctrl+c, the zone list being cleared at the top of the build, the composer already holding a TextLayout, and gpui's index_for_position / position_for_index both working in flat window space. inferred covers the join rules in the clipboard table and the five-pixel slop, neither of which has been tried against a real drag. not established: the cost of turning roughly seventy text runs into StyledText on a dense card, which nobody has profiled. The composer already uses one every frame, so the per-run cost is known to be affordable; seventy of them is an extrapolation. Figure 01's strings and proportions are hand-set, not captured from the running app.
