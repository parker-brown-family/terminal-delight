What deco is made of

Square corners

A radius says “soft, web, tap me”. Its absence says instrument, and it puts every edge on one grid.

One strategy value, corner = "square", and 141 hand-written radius calls stop mattering.

The twin rule

One line between two things reads as adjacency. Two lines with a gap reads as a boundary. It is deco’s signature and the cheapest way to make regions legible.

The header is banded by the rule under it, not by a fill — which is why row() and bar() are separate primitives.

The bracket

Four corner ticks mark the active row without filling it, so a dense list keeps its text contrast. A filled pill in a list of twenty is a stripe; a bracket is a pointer.

It also stops a task that already carries its own colour from being asked to wear two.

Tracked caps

Letterspaced small caps on labels only. Deco’s voice, and it separates furniture from content at a glance.

Applied over what the call site asked for, never instead of it — an already-uppercase label stays uppercase under the default skin.

deliberately absent

No bezels, no rivets, no gradients, no glow, no sunbursts, no chevrons, no HUD. The deco palette also turns every CRT effect off except a 0.14 vignette, which is a floor rather than an effect — it stops a flat near-black panel from reading as an unlit region of the desktop.

Three skins ship. console is the other reference screenshot — the operator console: dark planes, no panel borders at all, one rail on the row you are on. Its idea is separate-by-space-not-by-line, and killing every panel edge leaves only the lines that mean something.

How to switch, live

No relaunch, no file editing. --pid N aims at one window.

A misspelling is refused in the socket thread against a mirrored list — err no skin "decoo" — have: default, deco, console, theme — rather than queued and answered ok. A switch that reports success and does nothing is the failure this whole layer exists to refuse.

console is also the argument the layer has been making, as an artifact: it is a TOML file and nothing else. No Rust was added for it.

The cutover

Your live window — 20 panes, 16 of them agents, one of them the session writing this — moved onto the new build. Four read-only guards ran first, because the question is not “does it work” but “what does it cost if it does not”.

the guard that failed, and why it did not matter

The fourth guard in the written recipe asserts hyprctl activewindow names the client, because hl.dsp.window.close() takes the ACTIVE window. It failed: the active window was org.omarchy.screensaver, since you were away.

That ruled out the compositor route rather than the cutover. A client holding zero ptys can take a plain SIGTERM instead — TERM, never KILL, so the socket closes and the host sees a detach rather than a disappearance. Relaunch still goes through the compositor, so the new window gets Hyprland's environment and its own scope.

It is a known ticket rather than a surprise, and it is the reason a cutover is still a thing you schedule rather than a thing you do casually.

Findings

Two accents, not one, is what keeps a busy screen from going flat

Brass is structural — rules, brackets, frames, the colour of the furniture. Mint marks the one thing that is running. A single accent doing both jobs is why most dark themes collapse into one texture the moment a screen gets busy, and it is visible in the mockups above: the running pip is the only cool colour on the panel.

inferred From the reference screenshots and the palettes already in the repo, not from a measurement.

Recipes are the requirement; the token list is just bookkeeping

If a skin had to be hand-authored per palette, five palettes times three skins is fifteen files and the system has failed while technically working. Writing a skin as recipes over palette roles is the one product constraint that shaped the architecture — and it is what the two right-hand mockups above are demonstrating.

measured a_recipe_skin_follows_whatever_palette_it_is_given asserts it; the four mockups are four runs of the real resolver.

One default-look change was made on purpose

Two adjacent triangle glyphs in the bar header were drawn at text.alpha(0.75) and text.alpha(0.70). Both read ink_dim (0.70) now. Preserving a 5% difference between two neighbouring glyphs forever would have been preserving the disease that 59 distinct alphas are.

measured The golden test asserts token values, not call sites, so it stays true — which is why this is written down here instead of hiding behind it.

A helper that can drop its &Theme is the proof the tokens are right

bezel_btn_s — the chrome’s main button, 32 call sites — changed its palette parameter to &Skin and lost nothing. Every colour it used (accent, surface brightened, surface darkened, text) was already a token. hicon_s did the same.

measured That is a stronger signal than any token count: a vocabulary that can replace the thing it was derived from is complete, and one that cannot is a subset pretending.

The layer cost two tokens and one strategy across six surfaces

The plan set its own failure line at “more than two or three tokens across slices 1–3”. The cut added edge_strong and ink_off — both transcriptions of expressions that already existed at six and one sites — plus shine, which was a genuine gap: nothing in the original five strategies could say whether a surface is lit.

measured That is what moved the difficulty score from 8 to an honest 6.

What happens next

Two surfaces remain unconverted and they are the seam you will see: the overlays (usage, savings, find, plugins, the pickers) and the theme tray. Together they are 495 literals, and they were left last on purpose — the tray is the most idiosyncratic surface in the app, full of colour disks and swatch grids, and converting it early would have pushed a one-surface token into the shared vocabulary.

Say go and they land in one pass. Nothing else is blocked.

Why shape is not just more palette

docs/plans/chrome-skin/02-architecture.md

The case for folding it in

One file, one reload path, one picker. Themes already carry [colors], [effects] and [font]; adding [chrome] would be the obvious move, and it would have been less code.

Why it lost

The codebase already made this argument against itself. $TD_PALETTE exists so that an external theme system — an Omarchy theme switch — can retint the running terminal without deciding how much CRT you get. That is the same separation, one axis over. Welding shape to colour would mean a deco look could never be had on the green palette, and an Omarchy switch driving colour would silently drag the chrome’s shape along with it.

There is also a practical asymmetry: the palette is per-pane and shape is not. A window whose panes had different corner radii would read as broken rather than as configured, so the two cannot share a scope even if they shared a file.

What it costs

A second file to know about, and a resolution order to explain: the user’s own skin.toml wins, then the builtin the active theme named, then default. That is one more rule than “the theme decides”, and it is the price of the axis coming apart.

Why the buttons lost their glint

Skin::bezel · app/src/skin.rs

What it was

bezel_btn_s is the chrome’s main control — the split buttons, new-tab, the left bar’s adopt and +, and twenty-five more sites. It carried two box shadows: a white inset glint along its top-left inside edge, and a dark seat under its bottom-right. Plus a diagonal gradient on the face. That is a raised physical key, and it is a good one.

Why flat is not a preference

A glint is a claim: this object sits above the surface, catching a light from up here. A hard square edge is a different claim: this surface is a plane. A skin that has chosen the second cannot also make the first without the two contradicting each other in the same six pixels — which is why every serious instrument panel ever built is flat, and not because someone preferred it.

So the decision moved into the shine strategy, and the call site stopped having an opinion. Under gradient the button is exactly what it always was; under flat the edge carries the whole state. Eight lit keys in a row read as a texture; eight edged ones read as eight buttons, which is the thing a bar of controls actually needs.

The part worth noticing

The helper’s signature changed from &Theme to &Skin — and nothing was lost, because every colour it used was already a token: the accent at two alphas, the surface brightened, the surface darkened, the text. A helper that can drop its palette parameter entirely is the clearest evidence available that the token set is the right one. hicon_s did the same.

The two defects the probe found

terminal-delight skin --skin deco --theme deco

A warning the colour of the frame

The deco palette’s first draft put brass in ansi3 — the natural place for it, since brass is the yellow of that palette. warn resolves from the yellow slot. So a surface at 90% of its ceiling would have been painted in exactly the colour of the rules and brackets around it.

Nothing in the code looked wrong. It was two identical hex strings in the probe’s output, three lines apart.

The same collision, in a theme we already ship

field-command sets accent = "#8fa85f" and ansi[2] = "#8fa85f". Both olive, deliberately — it is a military field-kit palette and olive is the whole idea. With ok defaulting to ansi2, “healthy” was the accent.

This one says the recipe was wrong rather than the palette. A terminal palette very often points its accent at one of its own normal colours; asking it to keep those two distinct is asking for something it was never designed to provide.

What changed

State inks now default to the bright ANSI slots (ansi9/ansi10/ansi11) — further from the accent by construction, and better signals against a dark ground anyway. And no_state_ink_collapses_onto_the_accent_in_any_builtin_pairing checks every skin against every theme, because the version of this bug that matters is the one in the pairing nobody thought to look at.

Rejected, with reasons

docs/plans/chrome-skin/02-architecture.md

Recipes that can reference other inks

It would let a skin say “the rule is my panel, lifted” — genuinely expressive, and it turns resolution into a graph walk with cycles to detect and an error state to report from inside a hot-reload. Roles only. If a skin needs a derived-from-derived colour, it can write the two-step recipe out.

Chamfered corners

The cut corner is what a reader pictures when you say deco. gpui cannot cut a corner off a div — it needs a rendered path, and a path per chrome element is a rendering change rather than a token. Corner::Square plus brackets() gets most of the read for none of the risk.

Letter-spacing as a style property

gpui has none. Caps::Tracked therefore inserts U+2009 THIN SPACE between letters, in the string, behind Skin::caps — so exactly one place does it and a test checks it without needing a window. A real space would read as word breaks.

Per-pane skins

The palette is already per-pane, so the machinery exists. But a window whose panes had different corner radii reads as broken, not as configured.

Converting everything in one pass

1,656 literals across two files, in a render function of 7,400 lines, while four other branches were merging into it the same day. Staged adoption is only possible because the default skin is pinned to today’s look by a test — without that gate, a half-converted app would be a drifting one.

Why this order

The pane header before the mother bar, even though the mother bar is more visible: the pane header repeats once per pane — fourteen times on a working screen — so a shape change reads hardest there, and pane.rs is a far quieter file. Convert where the conflict cost is lowest while the vocabulary is still moving.

The theme tray late, despite being where a skin picker would live. It is the most idiosyncratic surface in the app — colour disks, swatch grids, a paint shelf — and the one most likely to need a token invented for it alone. Doing it early would push a one-surface token into the shared vocabulary.

What would say this was the wrong layer

  • Slices 1–3 add more than two or three tokens between them. The vocabulary was too small, and a token invented per surface is a constant with extra steps.
  • A slice needs a call site to branch on which skin is active. That is the one thing the vocabulary exists to prevent; the fix would be a missing strategy value in skin.rs, never a branch at the call site.
  • Nobody writes a third skin. Two skins is a refactor that paid for itself in tidiness — the layer was justified on the claim that the third one is cheap.
