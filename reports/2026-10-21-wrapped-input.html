Delete long terminal selections

In Terminal Delight, long typed commands can continue onto a second or third screen row. Selecting those rows and pressing Backspace now removes the selected text. The same build adds your word, line and whole-input shortcuts, with a brighter 650 ms fade for erased letters.

650 ms

Minimum erasure fade, with more light remaining later in the fade. Ordinary replacements still start at 220 ms. Zero turns both off.

Backspace takes the whole selection

The old code accepted selections on only one row. A real mouse-drag test reproduced your failure: the selected passage stayed, and one character at the caret disappeared. The new range follows the wrapped input. These native captures exercise the keyboard path in a real Bash editor.

Three shortcuts choose how much to erase

The shortcuts choose text from the caret position in the focused editor. Deleting a line takes both sides of the caret and includes its soft wraps. Clearing input also takes the other lines in a multiline draft. It keeps terminal history and sends no Enter or interrupt.

ShortcutAction
Shift+BackspaceDelete the previous word.
Ctrl+Shift+BackspaceDelete the entire current line.
Ctrl+Shift+Alt+BackspaceClear the entire editable input.
