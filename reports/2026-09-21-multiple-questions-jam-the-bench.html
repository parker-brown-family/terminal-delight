The bench cannot see a question it is not standing on

You asked for a navigator across the top and a work area that follows it. The navigator is the easy half — the picker's own tab bar is already parsed into labels, and the bench throws the labels away at the last step before drawing. The work area is the hard half, and it is hard for one reason: while a question is pending, the only copy of it that Terminal Delight can reach is the pixels on the screen, and the screen paints one question at a time. Your instinct about going async is the right shape. What it actually needs is a source for the whole round that exists during the ten minutes you are deciding.

Difficulty 6/10 — splits cleanly: the drawing half is a 3, the compose-then-submit half is a 7. Read time ~10 min.

Read first

  1. measuredThe whole round is in the transcript — ten minutes too late

    The ask you photographed is line 312 of pane 12's transcript, written at 14:09:06; its answer is line 313, at 14:19:10. Two adjacent lines, ten minutes and four seconds apart. Both questions, all six options, every description and a 659-character preview per option — all of it lands after you have already decided.

    What it would take: a source that exists while pending. One hook, or one MCP call from the agent.

  2. measuredThe navigator you want is parsed already, then discarded

    round_on_screen reads the picker's tab bar into Step { label, done } — "Ended state" done, "Orphans" open, plus Submit. round_progress then draws grey segments and the words "1 of 2 answered", and never draws a label.

    What it would take: render the labels you already hold. Half a day, no new plumbing.

  3. measuredIt does auto-advance. It takes eight seconds

    READING_WINDOW_MS = 8_000. After the bench types an answer into the pseudoterminal it deliberately stops reading the screen for eight seconds, so a picker still repainting is not misread as a fresh question. Click an answer and the bench sits on the old one for eight seconds, then jumps. That is what "jammed" feels like.

    What it would take: make the window end on evidence rather than on a clock.

  4. measuredTwo surfaces for one decision, and nothing joins them

    In your screenshot the agent had also declared a decision surface covering question one. So the bench held a rich Q1 (weights, IF WE DO, approve/reject) while the terminal had moved to Q2. They are unrelated rows that happen to be about the same thing.

    What it would take: a round key both halves carry, so a declared brief can dock to a live step.

  5. inferredAsync is what it already is — the choice is relay or compose

    Every click on the bench becomes keystrokes into the picker: Hit::Choose → nav_index → Dispatch::Keys → the pseudoterminal. The bench is a remote control on a one-second poll. What you are describing is the bench becoming the place the answer is composed, with the terminal driven once, at submit.

    What it would take: the bench holding the set (above), plus a rule for who wins when you type in both.

Where the change lands

Four frames. Nothing moves between them; each one adds a piece.

What the bench can reach

The async question

It is already async, which is the part worth getting straight before choosing. The agent is blocked on the picker for as long as you take, whichever face you use. What your message is really proposing is a change of role: the bench stops relaying each press and starts holding a draft of the whole round.

Four ways to get there

C — draw what is already parsed

recommended first · ~half a day

Labels in a navigator across the top, replacing the grey bar. End the blind window on evidence — the screen having changed — rather than on an eight-second clock.

Gets: your point 1, and most of point 2. Does not get: the other questions' text.

A — a hook publishes the pending round

recommended next · the real change

A PreToolUse hook on AskUserQuestion writes the tool's own input to a file the pane watches. Every question, every option, every preview, from before the picker paints.

Cost: one machine-global hook. Risk: unverified that it fires for this tool.

B — the agent declares the round

complementary, not a substitute

The agent presents one question surface carrying all the steps before it asks. Nearly what already happened in your screenshot — it declared a decision for step one and stopped.

Cost: nothing in the app but a join key. Limit: an agent that has not heard of the protocol gets nothing.

D — harvest by driving the picker

rejected

Press Tab through every step, read each screen, Tab back. It is the only way to get the whole set with no new plumbing at all.

Rejected: it moves a cursor you may be holding, and it writes to the terminal to perform a read.

What I need from you

Does the navigator replace the progress bar, or sit above it?

You have named tabs on top in your description. The bar underneath currently says "1 of 2 answered" in words, which the tabs would make redundant — but the bar is also the only thing that reads at a glance when a round has eight steps and the labels stop fitting.

Mine: replace it, and let the tabs scroll. Bring the bar back if a real round ever exceeds about five steps.

When you click a step you have already answered, what should it show?

The picker will happily Tab back to it and let you change the answer. The bench could do the same — or it could show it read-only, as a record of what you said. The first is more useful and the second cannot lie.

Mine: read-only until the content source lands (slice 3), then editable — because a step you can change but whose options the bench has not read is a button that guesses.

If you answer in the terminal while the bench holds a draft, who wins?

This is the row the case table leaves undecided, and it is the one genuinely new problem here. The picker is always right about what was committed, the draft is right about what you intended, and both of them are you.

Mine: the picker wins without asking, and the bench says out loud that it dropped a draft — naming which step. A surface that silently discards a decision you made is worse than one that never held it.

Is the eight-second blind window mine to change, or is it load-bearing for something I have not found?

Its comment explains the failure it prevents — a picker still repainting read back as a fresh unanswered question — but not why eight. Nothing else in the codebase references the constant except the state ladder.

Mine: it is a stand-in for an observation nobody had time to build, and slice 2 builds the observation.

Method, and what stays unknown

what was run, and where it stops

What was actually run

  • 120 transcripts under ~/.claude/projects/, newest first, scanned for AskUserQuestion tool-use blocks and their matching results: 43 calls, 19 with more than one question, 0 written to disk without their answer.
  • Pane 12's own transcript read directly for the ask in your screenshot.
  • The code read from origin/main via git show, not from this worktree — this checkout is 63 commits behind.

What is inferred rather than measured

  • That the harness buffers the ask until it is answered. Zero pending asks across 120 files is consistent with buffering, but it is negative evidence: every ask in those files was eventually answered, so an orphan would only appear if one were abandoned mid-flight. The code comment in derive.rs asserts the same thing, and the whole screen-reading subsystem exists because of it — nobody writes a pixel scraper when the data is on disk.
  • That a PreToolUse hook fires for AskUserQuestion. Not tested. Your settings.json has PreToolUse entries, but all of them match file and shell tools, so there is no existing log that would show it either way.

What stays unknown

  • Whether the picker repaints the whole tab strip when you Tab between steps, or only the changed tab. If only the changed tab, the labels the bench holds can go stale mid-round.
  • Whether Terminal Delight can watch a file cheaply enough to do this without another poll.
  • Nothing here was built or rendered by the app. The mockups are hand-drawn HTML, not screenshots.

How to prove each finding wrong

run these first, before changing anything

"The transcript has no pending question"

Confirm: start a pane, have it ask a multi-question round, and while the picker is open grep AskUserQuestion its transcript.
Invalidates it: the tool-use block is there before you answer. Then option A collapses to "watch the transcript", the hook is unnecessary, and slice 3 gets much cheaper.

"The navigator needs no new source"

Confirm: add the step labels to round_progress and watch a real two-question round.
Invalidates it: the labels come back empty or stale on the second step — meaning the picker does not repaint the strip on Tab, and the strip is a snapshot rather than a live reading.

"Auto-advance takes eight seconds"

Confirm: answer question one from the bench and time the next question appearing.
Invalidates it: it appears in about a second, which means the agent starts working inside the window and agent_state ends the suppression early — and the jam you saw is something else, most likely figure 1's two-unrelated-surfaces problem on its own.

"A declared decision and a live question are unjoined"

Confirm: present a decision surface and start a round in the same pane; check whether either surface carries a key naming the other.
Invalidates it: a join already exists somewhere I did not look, in which case slice 4 is mostly wiring rather than design.
