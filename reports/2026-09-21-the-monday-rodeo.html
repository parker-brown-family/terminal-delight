4

Select text in the composer with shift and a motion

PR 639 and 646 · the one that went a month with no way to highlight anything

Do: type two lines into the composer with shift+enter between them. Then: shift+← a few times, shift+ctrl+← for a word, shift+home, shift+↑. Press ctrl+c and paste somewhere. Press ctrl+a, then ctrl+z. Then press ← with no shift.

Pass: the highlight grows from where shift first landed, not from wherever the caret was last. ctrl+c copies the selection and does not interrupt your agent. ctrl+z undoes. A bare motion clears the selection and leaves the caret where it moved to — there is exactly one function that decides that now, and the caret cannot be placed past it.

5

Drag over a card and copy it off the bench

PR 621 · four ways out, and they are not the same chord as the terminal's

Do: drag across the prose of a response card. Then try each exit: middle-click into another window (the X11 PRIMARY selection is published on release), ctrl+shift+c, and right-click → Copy. Then drag across the comment rows on the right rail and do it again.

Pass: the highlight follows the pointer across runs and stops at the fence above; all three exits give the same text; ctrl+c is untouched and still interrupts a running agent. With no selection, ctrl+shift+c falls through to the terminal's own copy.

If a run is missing from the highlight, that is the failure worth writing down — relaunch with TD_SELDEBUG=1 and the window prints which run the pointer resolved to, and how many it knows about.

Three things only you can settle

Should a bench you are not looking at still hold a picker open?

Today the hook holds the agent's question only while the marker says the workbench is the face on screen and was written within four seconds. A round fired at 13:35 on the pane next to this one and was released as stale on a window that was very much alive.

My read: the four-second rule is right for a window that has died, and wrong for a pane sitting on the terminal face in a window you are using. Those are two different facts wearing one marker. inferred — I have one observation, not a measurement of the refresh cadence.

Text selection is merged, and its plan page still says awaiting approval.

The combined plan for dragging over the bench asked four questions — whether presses on the card body and rail rows may act on release, whether the composer was still wanted, whether verb chips are highlightable in passing, and what else belongs inside the fence. It shipped as pull request 621 with those questions open.

My read: answer them against what you see in item 5 rather than on paper, and let the gate close retroactively. The one that could still change code is the verb chips.

Do you want Codex asking rounds at all?

It is established that Codex can ask one — three questions, keyed by id rather than by question text. Reaching it costs you a trust prompt in Codex, and costs the adapter two small changes that are currently unreachable code.

My read: worth it only if you actually run Codex panes on the bench. If the bench is a Claude surface in practice, saying so closes an issue and stops two agents circling it.
