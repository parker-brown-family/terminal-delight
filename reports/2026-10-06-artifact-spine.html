Artifact Chip

You scrolled a long way up one pane today for a link its agent had handed you. This puts a chip in every pane's header carrying the name of the newest thing that pane's agent handed over, with the whole list one click below it.

difficulty 5 / 10 · one plan, one approval nothing built yet 4 calls for you

Where it stands

Click the chip's name and the artifact opens over that pane. Click ▾, or press Ctrl+Shift+M in the pane, for everything that agent has handed over, newest first. Rows come from the declare-deliverable verb and from the Deliverable: line agents print. Each is filed into that agent's workbench, so the list survives a restart.

This replaces the first draft's single list for the whole window. The store is the same either way, so a window-wide view can still be added later as a second reader.

Figures 04 and 05. The alternative is the verb alone, and asking every agent to call it.

Recommendation: yes, only a row that starts with Deliverable: and carries a link, never a Links table.

2 · Where is a handover kept?

Filed into the agent's bench as an artifact, or held in the window's memory, which forgets everything at a restart.

Recommendation: the bench. The chip and the pane's own ARTIFACTS shelf then read one store, and the files outlive the window.

3 · What does the chip say?

The newest handover's name, or a count in words such as 16 handed over.

Recommendation: the name. It is the thing you were scrolling for, and clicking it opens it.

4 · Build slice 1 now?

The chip, its list, and the verb filing into the bench. It needs answer 2.

Recommendation: yes, then slice 2 straight after.

Drawn from the code

  • 01: the header's controls, their order and the tuck widths (👤 at 470 px, EQ at 410, 🎨 at 360, 👓 at 264) are in the responsive-header block of pane.rs; the rule about numbers on the chrome is the comment under the face toggle there.
  • 03, the green boxes: declare_deliverable in mcp.rs sets one Option<Deliverable> per pane (pane.rs); the bench reads surfaces/<session>/<pane>/*.json (surfacefeed.rs), and its ARTIFACTS shelf is Shelf::Artifacts.
  • 05, the heal: row_flows_into_next in pane.rs joins a row to the next only when the next one starts with a non-space character.
  • The screen scans: latch_asked in pane.rs keeps the last thing you asked from the tail at 120 ms and a walk of the whole screen once a second, because the conversation is not in scrollback. The handover latch would ride the same two clocks.
  • Ages: attention::age_label counts seconds, minutes and hours and never days, so a day-old row reads 25h.

Method and limits

what this page did not establish

Nothing here was built or run. The code was read on main at the commit that retired a turn's response early (53f476d).

Figure 02 is reconstructed from a transcript, which is not what the chip will read. The chip will only know handovers it saw on screen or through the verb after it ships; the transcript stands in for that history.

Not measured: how often a Deliverable: line is never on screen at all, which happens if a reply arrives in one piece taller than the pane. The whole screen is read once a second, so any line that is drawn and stays a second is caught. How often it is missed is a hunch until slice 2 counts its own sightings against the transcripts.

Not measured: whether a chip fits every header in your layout. The widths come from the code's tuck thresholds and three panes' column counts, not from a running window.

A pane whose agent is replaced shows the new agent's handovers, because the bench follows the conversation. Not checked against a resumed session.

Codex panes were not looked at. Their transcripts live elsewhere and were not in the count; the screen rule applies to them unchanged.
