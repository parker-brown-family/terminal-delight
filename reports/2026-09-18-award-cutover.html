Why a narrowing no longer survives a restart

Nothing on screen ever offered the scope as a setting — it is a chip you press to concentrate, the way you would squint. Persisting it meant a window could open in a state you set days ago and no longer remember setting, with the tab you are in missing from the bar. It now lasts as long as the window does. Decisions I made while you were away

The scope chip filters the strip, rather than the strip growing a second control

The alternative was a strip that shows the active branch first and then fills the row with everything else. That is a bigger change to drag-and-drop, to the wrap gesture and to the drop caret, and it invents a control where one already existed and was already labelled. Reversing mine is one line: put tree::family back where tree::shown is called.

The effort dial falls back to the harness default rather than to a question mark

This is the one that argues with a rule you already have written down — unknown is not zero. I kept the rule and moved where it is said: the faint ink is the claim nobody chose this, and the word is the level the harness would run at untouched, which TD already asserts in Harness::default_effort. If you would rather have the question mark back for the never-told case, it is one arm of a match.

Nothing of yours was restarted, and no other agent's work was taken

Twenty-odd live agent panes are downstream of your window. Cutting the launcher over changes what the next window runs; killing the one you are in would have taken every one of them with it. The same reasoning covers the three worktrees: an uncommitted diff is a session's unfinished sentence, and committing it for them is how two agents end up owning one change.
