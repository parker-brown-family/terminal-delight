Figure and Ground

terminal-delight · chrome/phosphor-legibility · 181bec1 · 18 September 2026

Three words sit along the top of the workbench — overview, artifacts, decisions. Two of them were grey marks on a dark ground and the third was a glowing pill with something written inside it. Six inches to the left, in the same window, the OPUS · HIGH · END SESSION strip was perfectly readable, and the complaint arrived pointing at it:

compare to model and effort which look fine… dialing back the phosphor to the level of the model and effort buttons but like +10% more phosphor

So the question was not what is wrong with the tabs. It was what that strip does differently, given both of them are drawn by the same skin, out of the same tokens, four hundred lines apart in the same file.

What the strip does

It draws its value in th.text and lets the edge carry the colour. Every tab in the window went through Skin::ring instead, which paints the word in select, the border in select, the seat under it in select and two shadows around it in select.

What changed

The switch

TERM and BENCH were two chips with two pixels between them. Under the ring each reserved its own border, so one binary choice put four vertical edges on the pane header, and the lit half was a pill you could not read the word inside. It is one bordered track now, with the half you are on filled and carrying the control's whole phosphor budget, so throwing the switch moves the glow rather than lighting a second one.

What is not settled

  • The case of the shelf labels. overview at ten points would read better as OVERVIEW, and the skin already has a caps strategy — but it is off by default, so turning it on here would be a look change smuggled in under a legibility fix. Say the word and it is one line.
  • Tide and retro are palettes, not themes, and they live in ~/.local/share/terminal-delight/palettes/ rather than in the repo, so the automated floor cannot reach them. The argument that they are now fine is structural rather than measured: the label derives from the same role the terminal draws its own text in, so a palette that can show a prompt can show a tab. Worth a look on the real thing.
  • The thumb fill on the slider is 1.6× a seat. That number came from wanting the thumb to survive without a border of its own, and it is the one value here nobody has argued with.
