The plan

An agent never replaces a square you have open. Its artifact is filed instead, the agent counter in the top right gives a small bump, and the artifact's card goes to the top of a new artifact rail. Clicking the counter opens that rail where the agent wall used to open: one card per agent that has handed something over, newest first. Clicking an artifact takes you to its agent and opens it there, and the card stays. The agent wall keeps only Ctrl+Shift+A.

06Decisions

Does the hold apply to every pane, or only the one you're focused on?

You said your focused pane. A square on a pane you stepped away from, mid-read, is in the same position.

Every pane. An agent never replaces an open square, focused or not.

When does the counter bump?

On a hold only, or on every new artifact from any agent.

On a hold, plus a dot until the rail is opened. 37 artifacts a day would make every-artifact bumps noise.

What is a card called?

You said the agent's title. 27 of the 34 agent panes in this window are titled “shell”.

The project and the left-bar tab name. A second agent in the same tab adds its sticky note's title.

What happens to a card when its pane closes?

There is no agent left to go to, but its artifacts are still in its conversation's record.

The card goes with the pane in this version. Keeping closed agents is a later reader over the same record.

Whose agents are on the rail?

Each workspace runs its own window. One rail across windows needs a jump between windows that doesn't exist yet.

This window's panes. Each window has its own rail.

07Scope and order

What each picture is drawn from

The code. The counter's six cells, glyphs, 22 px height and dimmed zeros are render_agent_counter on dev/0.5.0, and its click sets mcp_menu, the wall's open flag. The replace-unless-unsaved behaviour is open_here in pane/doc.rs. The card chrome (radius 6, 3 px left edge) is the coordination panel's card loop. The jump to a pane is the attention spine's pending_jump. The replayed animation is eng-frame-in. The wall's chord is Ctrl+Shift+A, emitted as OpenAgentPanel.

Real data. The 37, 13 and 8 are counted from open_document calls in every Claude transcript written in the last 24 hours. The servo times, the card artifacts and their times are those calls. Tab names and projects are the left bar's, read through td-spy's tab-groups.py. “27 of 34” is list_panes at 9:45.

Mine. The counter's numbers, the cards' state colours, “7 earlier” (eight calls, not a count of the list), the bump's size and 450 ms, and every layout choice in the rail.

Method and limits

Nothing here is built or rendered by the app. The mockups are hand-drawn HTML at the code's sizes, and fonts and the compositor will change how they feel.

Which of the 37 opens replaced a square somebody was using is unknown: the agent's reply is the same either way, and TD keeps no log of what a square showed before. The sequence in figure 1 is the servo tab's real calls. That you were reading its earlier page at the time is an inference from the order you described.

Codex panes hand things over through the same printed line and the same verb, so the rail covers them unchanged. Neither was measured for this page.
