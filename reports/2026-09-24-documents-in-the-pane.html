Documents in the Pane

Terminal Delight is going to open the documents agents hand back — Markdown, HTML briefs, images — inside its own panes, instead of sending each one to a desktop window. This morning’s research brief ended on five questions about how, and your notes answered all of them. Alt+click floats a document over the pane in the glow TD’s menus use, Ctrl+Alt+click opens it beside the agent, and Ctrl+click still hands it to the desktop. Two answers pulled against each other: HTML should start as a picture of the page, and a decision brief must keep its comments and its save. A picture runs no code, so the brief’s own notes system cannot run inside it. The way through is to split the job. The engine supplies only pixels and the positions of things; TD draws the notes and writes them into the file itself, in the format the brief already uses. That split is also what lets an engine be unplugged, the modularity you asked for. This page lays out that architecture with a spike’s measurements; you approved it this afternoon, and the calls at the end are still open to a note.

Your notes, drawn as the screens they describe

Start with what you decided, because every screen below is one of your notes drawn. The first is the whole click map: Alt is TD’s modifier, so both new gestures carry it, and the old ones had to move out of their way without disappearing.

That last point is a collision the plan has to settle. Today Alt+click copies a whole line whenever TD reads it as a command or it starts with a URL — and a bare file:// link on its own line, the shape agents here are told to print, is exactly such a line. The proposed rule is that a document TD can draw under the pointer opens, anything else copies as it does now, and while Alt is held, the chip at the end of the line says which one the click will do.

Images and Markdown fit those screens without trouble. The brief is the case that pushes back.

A picture of a page cannot take a note

A decision brief does its annotation in JavaScript. When the page loads, its notes script walks the document, gives every figure, card and question an anchor id taken from its title, and hangs a note button on each; “save into file” copies the whole page with the notes baked into a small JSON block near the end, plus a plain-text map in a comment. None of that runs in a picture. So there were two ways to keep comments and save: embed a live browser engine now, against your note to start with an image, or have TD do the notes itself on top of the picture.

Doing the notes in TD sounds like a second notes system. The next section is why it is the smaller design, not the larger one.

The engine supplies pixels and positions, and TD owns the notes

The architecture builds up in three frames. The first adds the two placements to TD as it stands; the boxes from today never move.

The second frame puts something in the placements. One document view per open file, and three backends behind it. Images need nothing new. Markdown is the renderer lifted from markdown-delight, taking TD’s palette. HTML is where the modularity goes: a trait with one job, so a disappointing engine is swapped by configuration rather than rewritten.

The third frame answers the brief. The page engine hands back a picture and a list of where things are; the notes layer, which belongs to TD and not to any engine, draws the note buttons at those places and reads and writes the brief file.

That design rests on two claims a spike could test before any TD code: that the anchors come out the same as a browser’s, and that writing two regions of the file is safe.

The write is the part that could hurt a file, so here is exactly what it touches. Everything else in the brief — the prose, the pictures, the modals, the brief’s own script — is read and never written.

Two findings belong to the brief skill rather than to TD, and both are now issues on its repository. A browser that ever stored notes for a brief’s filename — even the empty set the delete-all button leaves behind — never shows the notes saved in the file again, and copies with the same name in different folders share that stored set; your Chromium holds 22 such entries. And a note containing </script> breaks the page’s own “save into file” and loses every note. Until the first is fixed in the skill, TD can only warn when it changes a brief that a browser may be shadowing. measured

The picture is the other half. The tallest brief would fit in one GPU texture, but it would hold 153 MiB of video memory while open, so the page is cut into bands and only the ones near the screen stay on the GPU.

The rest of the spike settled smaller questions the same way. Modals render on demand as their own images, about 150 ms each, opened by their id rather than through the page’s wiring — which also makes the six briefs whose modal buttons do nothing in a browser work in TD. The corpus carries ten different copies of the notes script, so the engine runs each brief’s own copy rather than a reimplementation, and a file with no notes block is shown read-only. And the live engine for later has a known gap: Chromium’s screencast delivers frames at 39 % of the pixels your screen needs. The full report is in the modal The spike, in full.

Six calls to argue with before any code exists

You approved this architecture this afternoon, before the spike reported, and the spike then confirmed both claims it rests on: anchors come out as a browser names them, and a save changes only the three regions it should. These are the calls still cheapest to overrule, each with the recommendation the plan was approved with; a note on any of them changes it while that costs a sentence rather than a rewrite.

1 · The split as a third pane face, with a shell idling behind the document?

The alternative is a real non-terminal pane in the split tree, which touches the tree’s leaf type and the sixty-three places the window walks it. The face costs one of four pane slots and a sleeping shell.

Recommendation: the face now. Revisit only if documents need to live in a tab of their own.

2 · TD’s notes layer is the only notes UI, in every engine?

With the snapshot engine there is no choice. With a live engine later, the brief’s own script could do the notes and TD would only catch the save. One UI keeps the look in one place; the cost is hiding a notes system that works, and following a format another writer keeps changing — the brief skill added a CONCUR stamp to every decision today, in a second JSON block, while this plan was being written.

Recommendation: yes, one notes UI, TD’s, guarded by shared fixtures: a few briefs with notes and concurs that both the skill and TD round-trip in their tests. A live engine later removes the drift entirely, which is a reason to want one.

3 · Save writes the brief in place?

In a browser, “save into file” downloads a copy you then move back. In TD it would rewrite the notes block in the original file, atomically, keeping a backup. The spike measured it: rewriting only the notes block, the concur block and the reader-notes comment added 240 bytes to a 135 KB brief and left every other byte identical.

Recommendation: in place, with the guards in the modal Risks to the file.

4 · The first engine needs a system browser?

Chromium is on this machine and is today’s HTML handler. A fresh install without it would get “open with the desktop” and a message instead of the page.

Recommendation: accept it for the first build; say so in the fallback message.

5 · Alt+click gives up copying on document lines?

The chip says which the click will do, and Copy link moves to the right-click menu. If that turns out to be the wrong trade, the rule flips to “Alt+click on the chip copies, anywhere else on a document path opens”.

Recommendation: ship the rule as drawn in figure 02 and watch for the flip.

6 · Anything to overrule before Gate 3 builds on it?

Gate 3 — the files, the types and their signatures, the call stacks and the test names — is being drafted against this architecture now, and comes to you with the slice plan.

Recommendation: a note on anything that looks wrong; no note keeps the plan moving.

The spike, in full

docs/plans/gui-in-the-tui/spikes/snapshot-engine.md

Run on this machine against the 119 HTML reports in ~/Work/reports and TD’s reports/, plus your saved brief; headless Chromium 151 driven by Playwright; a Rust probe on TD’s own wgpu revision. No TD code; every write to a throwaway copy.

  • Anchors: 53 in your saved brief (13 inside closed modals), all 9 of its note keys among them with matching titles; ids identical at five width and scale combinations, and at 968 and 600 wide in all 93 notes-bearing briefs. 7.4 ms warm.
  • Which notes script: 10 copies in the corpus — 3 skill releases in 81 briefs, 7 hand-edited forks in 12; one brief can show concurs. Today’s script would add anchors to 2 briefs.
  • Round trip: +240 bytes, 1.4 ms, other bytes identical; reopened with fresh storage the badges sit on the right 10 elements, one inside a modal; concurs round-trip through their own block.
  • Browser storage: an older stored map or an empty one beats the file; same-name copies share one entry; 22 notes: keys in your Chromium profile.
  • Page size: median 10,354 device px tall, 90th percentile 17,680, tallest 25,902; the GPU accepts 32,768 (Chromium’s own WebGPU reports 16,384).
  • Render: whole page 0.6–2.2 s cold, 0.3–1.9 s warm; visible band 45–73 ms plus ~14 ms to decode and upload; resize and re-anchor 70–93 ms. Chromium holds 265–570 MiB while a brief is open.
  • Modals: 285 in 75 briefs; ~150 ms each over the DevTools protocol; 30 scroll inside and need a taller viewport (~0.7 s).
  • Links and text: 79 links in 19 files; per-anchor and block text in under 10 ms.
  • No notes block: 26 files, 11 of them build partials; injecting the script and letting the page save fails; an explicit conversion works.
  • Live engine: screencast frames at 968 × 1400, CSS resolution; the page’s own save re-serialises the whole page (41–295 changed lines) and appends another reader-notes comment each time; an unanswered confirm() blocks the page.
  • Not established: why a brief lays out 17–29 px taller from its third load; whether headless text matches your desktop Chromium; how gpui’s atlas behaves with many 12 MiB tiles.

The file, with methods and scripts: docs/plans/gui-in-the-tui/spikes/snapshot-engine.md.
