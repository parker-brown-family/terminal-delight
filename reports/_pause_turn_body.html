Pausing a turn

You sent a turn on OPUS when you wanted MAX, and you noticed about four seconds in. Until this afternoon the bench had one answer for that, and it was END SESSION. The two dials sitting right beside it were grey — correctly, because a dial press types a slash command, and a slash command typed into a running turn waits in the harness’s line editor until that turn happens to end, so the change lands at a moment nobody chose. Ctrl+C in the TERM face did work. The bench simply never knew it had happened: a harness back at its prompt after an interrupt is pixel‑for‑pixel a harness that finished.

That is the whole of what was awkward here. Every other thing the strip says about an agent is read off its screen, and this one thing cannot be read at all — so the pane has to remember it. The strip now carries PAUSE TURN while a turn is running and RESUME TURN while one is stopped, the dials come alive the moment it stops, and in the six other states an agent pane can be in it draws neither. The word AGENT is off the far left.

  2. 2

    Reading your answer gets the pause too, and that was my call

    The eight seconds after the bench types into the terminal and before the spinner appears. You said “unless a turn is running”; this is the window where a turn has been sent and has not visibly started. It is the window you would actually notice the wrong model in, so I included it. Being early costs one interrupt arriving at a prompt, which clears a line.

  3. 3

    Paused outranks Finished, or your rule is not true

    A harness may ring its bell on the way back to the prompt, and the bench’s “done” sensor is that bell. Without the ordering in figure 04, an interrupted turn would read Finished and the strip would offer a resume on a turn that ended on purpose — the exact thing you forbade.

  4. 4

    The second copy of the question is gone, and the card’s navigator became pressable to pay for it

    The round steps — Morning / Weather / Free night / Sweet or sav — were clickable only in the pinned copy underneath. Removing the pin without moving that would have taken the step‑to‑step gesture with it. Both halves are in the same change.

  5. 5

    A pause does not survive a window restart

    Not built, deliberately. The latch lives in the pane, not on disk, so a relaunched window shows a paused pane as Idle and offers no resume. Saying so is cheap; storing it is a schema. Tell me if it matters and it becomes one.

Why the rung sits at the top

Paused is a new rung on a ladder that decides what one word at the top of the bench says. The height is not a preference — it is what the rung had to clear to ever be true.

What travels, and what puts the pause down

The question that was printed twice

Yours to settle

Does the pause belong in the eight seconds before the spinner appears?

My reading of “unless a turn is running” includes Reading your answer — you have sent something and the agent has not visibly picked it up. I recommend keeping it, because that is the window in which you notice the model was wrong, and the worst case is one interrupt clearing an empty prompt line. Cutting it is a one‑word change to turn_control.

Is Proceed with the turn. the sentence you want?

It lands in the transcript directly under the harness’s own [Request interrupted by user], so it is read by a person later as well as by the model now. I avoided “continue” because it collides with the slash command of the same name. One constant, RESUME_SAY, changes it.

Should ctrl+G and the button stay the same gesture?

Ctrl+G on the composer was already the aimed interrupt and now also sets the latch, so a turn stopped by the chord offers the resume exactly as one stopped by the button does. I recommend this stays — two gestures with one meaning leaving the pane in two states is the same shape of bug as two renderers drawing one question.

Should a pause survive a window restart?

It does not today. A relaunched window reads a paused pane as Idle and offers no resume, which is a wrong answer rather than a missing one — though a harmless wrong answer, since you can type. I recommend leaving it until a restart with panes mid‑turn is a thing that happens to you; storing it means a schema and a migration.

Method and limits

what was and was not established

What was run

  • cargo clippy --locked -- -D warnings — clean. This is the gate CI runs; it does not lint test targets.
  • cargo fmt --all — clean.
  • cargo test --locked — 1500 pass. Two failures in paneident also fail on a clean checkout of main: they walk /proc on a live machine with other agents running.
  • Five mutations, each reverted. See the fourth modal.

What was not

  • Nothing here has been seen running. No window was launched, no screenshot taken, no pane photographed. The pictures are drawn from source.
  • The width is unchecked. PAUSE TURN is eleven characters joining two dials and END SESSION on one row. On a narrow pane that row may wrap or clip, and only the real thing can say. Dropping the word AGENT bought back some of it.
  • The interrupt is trusted, not verified. One 0x03 stopping a Claude Code turn and leaving the session alive is how the existing ctrl+G behaves and is what the code has always claimed; it was not driven under a pseudoterminal for this change.
  • The pause does not persist. A relaunched window reads a paused pane as Idle.

What would invalidate the main claim

The claim is that a paused pane is distinguishable from a finished one. It would be wrong if a harness came back from an interrupt still printing its spinner rows for more than a second — the !thinking guard would hold the bar at Working and the resume would never appear. Check by pausing a long turn and watching the bar; if it does not reach Paused within about a second, the sensor and the latch disagree and the guard is the thing to revisit.

The first run of this was wrong, and in the way that matters

The script classified a result by grepping for a compile error before it looked for a failing test — and cargo prints error: test failed, to rerun pass … when a test fails, so all five mutations came back did not compile while every one of them was being caught. A generic check placed ahead of a specific one reads every outcome as the generic case. The order is fixed and the table above is the second run.
