Telemetry cache

The planner reads the same satellite telemetry thousands of times a launch day, and every miss is a round trip to the ground station. I replayed one launch day against both eviction policies.

measured on a replay2 decisions waiting5-minute read
Verdict

Evict by recency, with a 60-second cap on staleness. Recency kept 90.8% of reads in cache with the cap, against 78.2% for a five-minute age limit, and no reading older than a minute ever reaches the planner.

01Read first

  1. Recency wins by thirteen points. 91.4% hits against 78.2% on 412,000 replayed reads. measured
  2. Plain recency serves stale readings. During a quiet pass it handed the planner a reading 14 minutes old. measured
  3. A 60-second cap costs 0.6 points. The cap evicts anything older than a minute; the hit rate falls to 90.8%. measured

02What the replay showed

03Decisions

1 · Evict by recency or by age?

Recency keeps thirteen more reads in a hundred; age keeps every reading under five minutes without a second rule.

Recommendation: recency. Agree and I switch src/cache.rs to it.

2 · Cap staleness at 60 seconds?

The planner treats telemetry older than a minute as suspect, and plain recency served one 14 minutes old.

Recommendation: yes, at 60 s, for 0.6 points of hit rate.

How the replay was run

cargo bench --bench cache

The day

412,000 telemetry reads captured from one launch day, replayed in order at recorded timing against each policy with a cold cache of 4,096 readings.

What was measured

Hit rate, and the age of every reading served. Each policy ran three times; the spread between runs was under 0.1 points.

What would prove this wrong

A second launch day where recency's lead falls under five points. Step 3 of the path is that check.
