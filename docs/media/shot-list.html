The shot board

Forty candidate clips mined from the feature catalog, each one scored on whether a stranger would understand it in four seconds without narration — and on who has to be at the keyboard. Three are filmed, all of them driven by an agent over a socket with nobody in the room. Seventeen more are tagged the same way, though a few wait on the two ctl verbs named below; the rest need a hand on the keys.

The loop is review → post → mark where it went. A filmed shot carries its own clip: hit review, watch it, then light up the platforms it has gone out on. Marks, ticks and notes are saved to the board itself, so they survive a reload and come back when you ask Claude to read them.

Why the driver column exists

An agent on this box can drive the ctl socket and nothing else. It cannot click — Hyprland's Lua dispatch offers cursor.move but no button press, and there is no ydotool — and synthetic keystrokes via wtype did not reach the terminal even with the window focused and active. So every shot below is labelled by what actually drives it.

The staging rig is real and repeatable: scripts/demo-stage.sh up puts a throwaway instance (TD_SESSION, so it never restores your live panes) on a spare workspace as a float, at 940×880 logical — which gpu-screen-recorder writes as 1504×1408 physical pixels at a true 60fps. scripts/stage-clip.sh performs the gesture on cue. Teardown restores your workspace and cursor.

Two ctl verbs would move roughly six shots from "needs a click" to "agent can drive": select a tab, and close the front panel. A fresh instance opens on the MCP control screen, and nothing on the socket dismisses it — which is what currently keeps the terminal panes themselves off camera.

Driver Tier State connecting

Ticks and notes are saved to this board itself, not to one browser — they survive a reload, a different machine, and a new session, and Claude can read them back without anyone pasting anything. Say read my notes on the shot board and the notes come back by shot. The indicator above the list says synced when the store is connected and saved in this browser when it is not — the fallback for a copy opened straight off disk.

Voicing a shot happens off this page. A microphone take has to land on the machine that holds the master, aligned to it, so the workshop is local: node scripts/td-shorts.mjs serves docs/media/say.html at 127.0.0.1:8901, where a shot plays while you say its script over it. The take is trimmed to frame zero, mixed under the picture and padded to a 1080×1920 short in docs/media/to-upload/. Then post it, and light the platform up here.

The players below hold 760px review copies, inlined so a shot can be judged from a phone. The masters — 1504×1408 at 60fps, and what actually gets posted — stay in docs/media/clips/, named under each player. Reshoot with scripts/demo-stage.sh and scripts/stage-clip.sh, then run scripts/build-shot-board.sh to re-inline the review copies.

Post the mp4, not the gif: the same 7-second take is 445 KB as h264 and 2.5 MB as a gif, and the gif looks worse.
