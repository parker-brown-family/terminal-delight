Terminal Delight's keys come in three kinds. Alt with an arrow or a letter, and anything with Ctrl+Alt, belongs to the window: moving between panes, splitting, closing. Ctrl+Shift with a letter belongs to the pane: copy, paste, new tab, the panels. Everything else goes to whatever is on top, and underneath everything is the terminal.

So the window keys work the same whether a pane is showing its terminal or its workbench, and a shell never receives them. Ctrl+C always reaches a running program, and Esc closes the popup on top before a terminal ever sees it.

Technical lists every key, surface by surface.

Where a keystroke goes

A window-wide panel gets first look: Esc closes the popup on top, and while the Git coordination inspector or the attention queue is open it keeps the keys it uses. After that, a keystroke is checked in this order, and the first taker keeps it: F1, then Alt+K, then window chords, the paint overlay, open menus, the focus reader, pane chords, a sticky note being written, a rename box, a floating document, the document face, the workbench, and last the terminal.1

Window chords are Ctrl+Alt+anything, and Alt with an arrow or R V H W K L G. Ctrl+↑ and Ctrl+↓ are the window's too, but only on a Claude Code or Codex terminal with no box being written in and no document or workbench showing; everywhere else they reach the program. Pane chords are Ctrl+Shift with T C V K A B N M Z U Y D G F; Ctrl+W, Ctrl+F and Ctrl+X; Ctrl+Insert and Shift+Insert; the four size chords; and Alt+A, Alt+S, and Alt+Backspace while a note is stuck on the pane. Alt+B and Alt+F stay with readline. A box with a caret over the terminal (a note in a document, a sticky note, a rename) keeps the paste chords for itself.

In 0.5.0, Ctrl+W is a pane chord only while a document floats over the pane, and Ctrl+Shift+W joins the pane chords. A floating document also claims Ctrl+↑ ↓ and Ctrl+Shift+↑ ↓, so while one is up the shell doesn't get them. Ctrl+Shift+Alt+Backspace is the one Ctrl+Alt chord the window leaves alone: it goes to the line you're typing.2

Your messages to an agent

Ctrl+↑in a Claude Code or Codex pane, open CHAT HISTORY over the pane at the last thing you typed; again for the one before
Ctrl+↓your next message; past the newest, back to the live chat

It reads the conversation from the agent's transcript, with its replies in view, and sends nothing to the agent. In any other program Ctrl+↑ and Ctrl+↓ reach the program as usual. With the focus reader open on an agent's pane, the same keys step through your messages there.

What would prove this wrong

Every row in a table here is read by the Omarchy key sheet, so a key that does something other than its row says is wrong in two places. The claim this page rests on: a window chord does the same thing on the terminal and on the workbench, and a shell never receives it.
