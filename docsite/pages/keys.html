Terminal Delight's keys come in three kinds. Alt with an arrow or a letter, and anything with Ctrl+Alt, belongs to the window: moving between panes, splitting, closing. Ctrl+Shift with a letter belongs to the pane: copy, paste, new tab, the panels. Everything else goes to whatever is on top, and underneath everything is the terminal.

So the window keys work the same whether a pane is showing its terminal or its workbench, and a shell never receives them. Ctrl+C always reaches a running program, and Esc closes the popup on top before a terminal ever sees it.

Technical lists every key, surface by surface.

Where a keystroke goes

Checked in this order, and the first taker keeps it: F1, then Alt+K, then window chords, the paint overlay, open menus, the focus reader, pane chords, a sticky note being written, a rename box, a floating document, the document face, the workbench, and last the terminal. Window chords are Ctrl+Alt+anything, and Alt with an arrow or R V H W K. Pane chords are Ctrl+Shift with T C V K A B N Z U Y D G F, plus Ctrl+W, Ctrl+F, Ctrl+X and Alt+S. Alt+B and Alt+F stay with readline.

Paint overlay

Raised by terminal-delight ctl paint on. A set's first letter paints it; arrows walk the panes; d hands the pane back; z / Shift+Z turn the shelf; f jumps to favourites and Shift+F stars; Esc folds it. Other letters are swallowed, and chords with a modifier pass through. Themes has more.

On the desktop

  • Ctrl+Alt+T on GNOME opens Terminal Delight, once scripts/install-hotkey.sh has bound it. The app itself does nothing with that chord.
  • Super+Alt+T on Hyprland or Omarchy moves the focused terminal, and the agent in it, into Terminal Delight, once scripts/install-send-hotkey.sh has bound it.
  • Super+Alt+P on Omarchy raises the paint overlay, once Terminal Paint, the 🎨 in the Omarchy bar, has bound it. The app answers terminal-delight ctl paint on without it.
  • Super+W on Omarchy closes the whole window: that's the desktop's key, not Terminal Delight's.
