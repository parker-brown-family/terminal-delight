A tab holds up to four panes, tiled. Every pane is a real shell with its own header, and every divider can be dragged.

Alt+V splits the pane you're in side by side; Alt+H splits it top and bottom. The new pane opens in the same directory. Alt and an arrow key moves between panes. Drag a pane by its title to move it: onto another pane, onto a tab, or out of the window into a window of its own.

Closing doesn't end anything straight away. Alt+W closes a pane and Ctrl+W a tab, and either one goes to the bay at the foot of the left bar with its shells still running. Ctrl+Shift+Z brings it back. Closing the last tab is the one close that quits.

On 0.5.0, Ctrl+W closes the document floating over a pane, and closing the tab moves to Ctrl+Shift+W.

To make text bigger, press Ctrl+= or Ctrl+- in a pane, or hold Ctrl and turn the wheel over exactly what you want sized: a pane's text, its header, or the window's chrome.

Technical covers the header, closing, sizing, the tab strip, the top bar and the footer.

Each tab is a tiling tree. A split replaces the pane you're in with two panes at half each, the new one on the right or below, and hands it your working directory and your focus. A second split within 200 ms is ignored, so a held key doesn't fill the tab.1

Splitting and moving

KeyDoes
Alt+VSplit beside: a new pane on the right.
Alt+H or Ctrl+Alt+DSplit below: a new pane underneath.
Alt+← → ↑ ↓Move focus to the pane in that direction. Nothing happens at the edge; it doesn't wrap.

Alt+KFlip the pane between its terminal and its workbench.

Alt+ROpen the focus reader on the focused pane.

The split buttons do the same. They sit in the window's bottom-right corner, each a small square with the half filled where the new pane will open: the side-by-side one at the foot of the right-hand column, the stacking one at the right end of the bottom edge. Hover says split → or split ↓.

A tab takes four panes at most; a fifth split is refused. Layouts saved before that limit can still hold up to eight. A pane dragged onto a full tab, through the tab strip or onto its task in the left bar, is refused and the layout stays exactly as it was; the task row shows red while you hover. Rearranging panes inside their own full tab is always allowed.

Drag a divider to resize; it stops at 15 % and 85 %. There is no key for resizing a pane. Drag a pane by its header title: onto another pane, it splits that pane on the side you drop nearest; onto the outer edge of a region, it re-frames the whole region; onto a tab in the strip, it joins that tab; past the edge of the window, it opens a new window with a fresh shell in the same directory, resuming the same Claude or Codex session. A running shell can't move between processes, so the new window starts its own. Tearing a pane off needs more than one pane in the window.

In the Clean design on 0.5.0, under the TERMINIMALIST skin or another skin with text controls and no boundaries, panes side by side meet on one shared hairline instead of a dark gutter, and dragging the hairline still resizes them. The CRT designs and the other skins keep the gutter.6

Closing and bringing back

A closed pane or tab is held, not ended. Its shell keeps running in the bay at the foot of the left bar for an hour, or four hours if it had two or more panes. The bay holds ten things.2

You closeWhat happens
A pane, with Alt+W or its ×The split collapses onto its neighbour; the pane goes to the bay. A pane alone in its tab closes as a tab.
A tab, with Ctrl+WAlways asks first: CLOSE TAB?, Enter to close, Esc to cancel. Terminal Delight keeps Ctrl+W, so the shell never sees it as delete-word.
A tab, with its × in the stripCloses a one-pane tab straight away and asks first for more.
The last tabAsks, then ends its shells and quits: “notes” is the last tab — closing it ends its shell and quits. This can’t be undone.

On 0.5.0 the chords split by size. Ctrl+W puts away the document box floating over the pane, one box per press, as its ✕ and Esc do; with no box open it reaches the shell or agent as delete-word again. Ctrl+Shift+W closes the tab and still asks first.6

Ctrl+Shift+Z reopens the newest thing in the bay, and pressing it again walks further back. A reopened pane says where it landed: back where it was, back in its tab, in a new seat or back as its own tab. When a shell exits by itself, its pane is removed, and the app quits when the last pane anywhere is gone.

The pane header

The title reads ▸ MODE · label. The mode is SHELL, CLAUDE, CODEX, LOCAL for a local agent you configured, REMOTE, UNKNOWN, or the name of the program running. The label is the name you gave the pane, or the file name when it shows a document, or the shell's own title. Double-click or right-click the title to rename it (up to 24 characters), and drag it to move the pane. Left of the title is the pane's logo; click it to pick one.3

On the right, in order:

  • The face switch, TERM · BENCH, or DOC · TERM · BENCH on a pane showing a document. When the bench is holding answers for the agent it adds N answers waiting.
  • ⌁ AGENT, or ⌁ alone on a narrow pane, which opens the agent launcher for this pane, as Alt+A does.
  • On an agent pane that has handed something over, the name of the newest handover, with a dot until you open it, and ▾ for the list of everything that agent handed over; Ctrl+Shift+M opens the same list. A narrow pane shows ▤ artifacts instead of the name.
  • 🎨 for the pane's theme and the display icon for its dials. On a narrow pane these move into the ⋯ menu as 🎨 Theme and 📊 Display.
  • The status word: ❓ your turn, ✘ blocked, ✔ done, exited or live, in that order of precedence.
  • × closes the pane. The face switch and × never fold into the ⋯ menu.

The header shows no grid size and no bare numbers: anything it counts says what it counts. A pane marked off-limits to agents from its right-click menu wears OFF-LIMITS (see MCP server).

On 0.5.0 an agent pane's header leads with the model and effort its transcript names, such as OPUS 5.5 MAX · label, instead of CLAUDE; until the transcript names a model, the program's name stands in, and an effort it never named is left off. The header learns a /model switch within a minute. A pane nobody has named shows its pane number where it used to say shell, on the header and everywhere else that names a pane. UI CONFIG can put each header control away.6

Documents in a pane

A pane can show a Markdown file, an HTML page, an image, a video or a PDF in place of its terminal; that is its DOC face. Alt+click a file path to float it in a square over the pane, Ctrl+Alt+click to open it in a new pane beside you, Ctrl+click to open it with your desktop's app, and Shift+click to show it in the file manager. A document pane comes back after a restart on the same page. Documents has the keys, video and PDF controls, and ctl doc.4

Editing what you type

Select part of the line you are typing, with Ctrl+Shift+← → or the mouse, and Backspace or Delete removes all of it; Ctrl+X cuts it. A selection in scrollback, or in a full-screen program, is left alone.

On 0.5.0 a selection can be erased even when your input wraps onto several rows or holds line breaks, and three more chords edit it: Shift+Backspace deletes the previous word, Ctrl+Shift+Backspace the whole current line, and Ctrl+Shift+Alt+Backspace everything you have typed. Full-screen editors keep their own keys. The workbench composer takes the same chords, with undo, and the F1 help lists them.6

The tab strip

The tab strip is on screen while the left bar is hidden. It reads the active tab's project, then that project's groups, then the tabs of the group you're in. A group chip goes to that group's first tab, and the project chip to the project's first tab outside any group. With the bar open, which is the 0.4.0 default, the tabs are listed in the bar instead.

On 0.5.0 a new window opens in Clean, which shows the tab strip and keeps the left bar shut. The project chip opens a switcher listing every top-level project and group; see Left bar.

The top bar

From the left: the app's mark and the Git signal, then the tab strip when the left bar is shut, then the agent counter at the right end. Drag the bar to move the window.1

Git signal
One line covering every project in the window, such as Git · No new findings. Click it, or press Alt+G, for the inspector.
agent counter
▶ working, ‖ blocked, ✕ error, ✓ finished, ○ idle, ? unknown, each with a count. Its border turns amber when an agent needs you. Click it for the agent wall.

On 0.5.0 the counter opens the Threshing Floor instead. When the top bar holds nothing (the mark, the Git signal and the counter all hidden by Clean's UI set, the tabs in the left bar and no window buttons) it folds to a hairline, so the panes sit as far below the frame as from the sides.6

Not yet

  • No keyboard resize for the panes themselves.
  • The bay does not survive the window closing, which is why closing the last tab still ends its shells.

What would prove this wrong

If a pane you closed with Alt+W comes back with Ctrl+Shift+Z as a fresh shell instead of the one you left, with its history gone, the bay has failed at the only thing it promises. And if a pane dropped on a full tab changes the layout at all, the cap has failed.
