When a pane is too small to read, press Alt+R. A panel opens across most of the window, everything behind it frosts over, and the pane's text is laid out again at the pane's own letter size across the whole panel, so a narrow pane opens with as many columns as the panel holds.

What it shows depends on the pane. On a Claude Code or Codex pane, it reads the conversation from the agent's own transcript: your messages and its replies whole, each tool call one row, with the agent's input box live at the foot. On a pane showing a document, it shows the document. On a shell, it shows the scrollback, joined where the terminal wrapped it. A chip in the header says which.

Your typing still goes to the pane, so you can read an agent's answer at a comfortable size and reply without closing anything. The A──A slider, or Ctrl and the wheel, sizes the letters. Esc, or a click on the frosted area, closes it.

Technical has the sources, the keys, the size and the backdrop.

The panel is 80% of the window each way, at least 320 by 240, with the header 👓 FOCUS · <pane title>, a chip naming what it reads, an Inherit theme switch, the A──A size slider and esc to close. Opening it on another pane replaces the one open. On a pane showing its workbench, Alt+R reads the terminal behind it.1

A pane showing a document lends it to the reader, which draws the pane's own document view at the panel's size: its notes, links and place stay the pane's, and the keys still page it. A document reads flat, so Inherit theme isn't offered there.1

A conversation

Your messages sit under YOU, the agent's under its own label, CLAUDE or CODEX. A reply's Markdown is drawn as text: headings bold, lists with their markers, code behind a gutter, tables as aligned columns. Each tool call is one row, ⚙ Read app/src/doc.rs → 40 lines, and a click opens or closes its output. Thinking, background notifications and command output nobody typed are left out, and a divider marks where the conversation was compacted. A long tool output is kept to its first 400 lines or 64 KiB, with a count of the rest.2

Under the conversation, the pane's own status line and input box are drawn live, so the turn in flight stays visible and what you type reaches the agent. A conversation with nothing in it yet says Nothing has been said here yet.

On a Claude Code or Codex pane, Ctrl+↑ and Ctrl+↓ open the same conversation inside the pane itself, under CHAT HISTORY · <pane title>, landing on your previous or next message. Stepping past your newest message, Esc, or switching tab returns to the live terminal. Nothing is sent to the agent while you move. In every other program the two keys reach it as usual.3

It opens at the newest line and follows output. Scrolling up stops the following; coming back to the bottom starts it again.

Size

The reader lays text out at the pane's own letter size, across the whole panel, and long lines wrap to its width. The A──A slider and Ctrl+wheel move the letters from 0.5× to 2.5× of the pane's, and the size goes back to 1× each time the reader opens. A pane in text crawl, which never wraps, is fitted into the panel instead. A short read sits in the middle of the panel.1

Inherit theme

Off by default. On, the reading area is warped and glared like its pane, at the pane's warp; the header stays flat. The choice is kept with the session. A pane in text crawl keeps its crawl font and centring in the reader but isn't tilted, wrapped or selectable.

The backdrop

Everything outside the panel is dimmed and blurred by the same renderer pass as the warp: 32 samples spread in a golden-angle spiral, a sharp rounded edge around the panel, and a faint cool tint, easing in over about 220 ms. While the reader is open, no pane is warped. The conversation inside a pane, from Ctrl+↑, has no backdrop.

Taking Alt+R means the shell's own Alt+R, readline's revert-line, doesn't reach it.

What would prove this wrong

Open the reader on an agent pane whose chip says TRANSCRIPT · live. If any message in it came from a different session than the one running in that pane, the rule that a transcript is read only when it is matched for certain has failed.
