Terminal Delight takes its colours from your desktop. Press Ctrl+Shift+D on a pane, or click 🎨 at the bottom right for the whole window, and the painter opens in three areas: Theme, Style and Rules.

THEME shows one tile for every theme Omarchy's own picker shows, drawn in that theme's colours. Click one and the pane or the window wears it. Switch on INHERIT under Rules instead, and the window follows whatever theme the desktop is wearing, repainting when you change it there.

Style holds the DESIGN row, which sets the finish rather than the hue: how much CRT texture and glow, and which font. Five designs are built in, from the light quiet to the loud intrusive. The sixth, custom, is your own theme.toml, reloaded as you save it.

A fresh install opens flat, in the Terminal Delight palette, with the CRT off.

A first launch opens in the Clean design instead, with no tube and no light anywhere. Design and skin move independently, and each design remembers how you tuned it.

Technical has the painter area by area, the designs, the paint overlay and the theme file.

A pane's painter opens from Ctrl+Shift+D and paints that pane. The window's opens from 🎨 at the bottom right and paints the outer, the frame the panes sit in, along with every pane that follows it. Hovering any choice explains it.1

The paper comes from the theme. contrast is a vivid hue apart from the accent, and the default; accent is the theme's accent; mute is neutral paper from the text and surface colours. The ink stays readable at 4.5:1 or better on every theme.

ansi keeps the colours programs send. mono draws them as shades of the text colour. theme keeps their hues but turns them around the accent.
syntax
Colours text the program left plain: keywords and paths for code, callouts, tool calls and links for agentic, levels and timestamps for logs, headings and emphasis for markdown.

A photographic negative of every colour, the sixteen ANSI ones included. Effects stay as they are.

The window's painter gains a fourth area, UI CONFIG, which hides pieces of the chrome. With its every option switch off, each row of the painter shows only its selection until you hover it for about three-quarters of a second or click it.4

The tile of the theme actually being worn gets a thicker border and a faint phosphor glow, and under Chalk more depth. It follows the theme the pane or window is drawing, so it marks the desktop's theme while INHERIT is on.

Themes

The THEME tiles, and the paint overlay's OMARCHY THEMES shelf, offer exactly the list Omarchy's picker shows: every theme installed under /usr/share/omarchy/themes, $OMARCHY_PATH/themes, ~/.local/share/omarchy/themes and ~/.config/omarchy/themes, a later folder shadowing an earlier one of the same name, minus any named in ~/.config/omarchy/hidden-themes. Installing, hiding, removing or editing a theme shows up within a second. A theme that ships only an alacritty.toml loads too. TD_OMARCHY_THEMES replaces the folder list.2

A theme's colours are converted the way Omarchy converts them for its terminals, so a pane painted tokyo-night shows ANSI colours exactly as every other tokyo-night window does. Painting one replaces the design's colours and keeps its texture. On a machine without Omarchy, Terminal Delight offers its own copies of Omarchy's 22 stock themes and of the Terminal Delight palette. A pane wearing a theme that has since been uninstalled keeps it if Terminal Delight carries a copy, and otherwise goes back to its design's colours.

INHERIT

On the window, INHERIT links the outer and every pane following it to the theme Omarchy has selected, and repaints them when the desktop switches theme. It reads ~/.local/state/omarchy/current/theme/colors.toml, or ~/.config/omarchy/current/ on an older Omarchy. While it is on, colours you set by hand are set aside; turning it off puts them back. Where nothing can be read it shows INHERIT · unavailable.2

A design is a theme file: colours, the CRT [effects] and a font. The texture only shows while the CRT switch is on.

A design that asks for a skin brings it only while you haven't picked one from the SKIN row; see Skins. Two designs are gone in 0.4.0: a pane saved on Field opens on Tactical, keeping its palette and gauges, and one saved on Gamba opens on custom.1

The DESIGN row is clean ○, deco, hacker and intrusive. Clean replaced Quiet, and Tactical is retired: a saved Quiet opens as Clean, a saved Tactical as Hacker. custom is no longer offered, though theme.toml still loads and a window already wearing it still shows its tile.4

Every design except Clean asks for the ops skin and the Standard UI set; Clean asks for the clean skin and the Clean set. Picking a design, resetting one or painting the desktop never changes the skin: a window that never picked one keeps the skin it was drawing. A right-click on a design offers Reset … to as designed and Undo.

A fresh window

A new install's window wears custom under the Terminal Delight palette, with the CRT off, theme program colour, agentic syntax and contrast sticky notes. Its gauges open at brightness −24, contrast +25, colour +50, menu bar 85% and text size 74%. Your theme.toml starts as a copy of hacker, so switching the CRT on gives that design's tube. An install upgraded from an earlier version keeps its look and its tube. Every new pane follows the window.1

A new window opens in Clean, with ansi program colour, mark syntax and INHERIT on. UI CONFIG's use this window makes the window you're in the template for new ones instead.4

Looks that remember

Each design keeps the changes you make while wearing it. Trying another design never writes to it, and picking it again brings it back as you left it. A dot on a design's button means it carries changes.4

What a design remembers is its look dials (the CRT switch, warp, glare, roll, crawl and its angle and depth, phosphor, letter glow, the inactive-terminal dials and the linger times) and, for the window, its UI CONFIG choice. Sizes, the picture gauges, the palette, program colour, syntax, sticky-note paper and the skin are yours in every design. In the window's GAUGES, a dot beside a dial's label means it differs from the design, and clicking the dot sets it back. Right-click the label to pin the dial to every design, and again to remember it per design. A design you never touched looks as designed. The memory is saved with the window as [looks].

Paint

terminal-delight ctl paint on raises a paint overlay over every pane, with a card hanging from the top edge for the outer, in every Terminal Delight window on the Hyprland workspace; off and toggle do what they say. The app binds no key for it; bind the command in your desktop.3

KeyDoes
a letterPaints the theme with that initial onto whatever is aimed at; pressed again, the next theme with that initial

While the overlay is up, Enter never reaches the pane, and Tab, Space, Backspace and Delete are ignored. The legend under each card names only the keys that work on this desktop. Stars are kept in favourites.toml.

The theme file

~/.config/terminal-delight/theme.toml is the custom design. It is written as a copy of hacker on first run, checked every 300 ms, and reloaded on change; a file that no longer parses prints theme reload error (keeping current): … and the current theme stays. TD_THEME points at a different file, and then nothing is written on first run. Hovering custom in the painter shows the path and ▸ Open in editor. The built-in designs' files are in app/themes/ in the repository.1

name = "mine"
icon = "▲"
skin = "deco"            # optional: the skin this design asks for

[colors]
bg      = "#03100a"
surface = "#071a10"
text    = "#86efac"
accent  = "#22c55e"
faint   = "#14401f"
cursor  = "#4ade80"      # optional, defaults to accent
human   = "#f0abfc"      # optional: the colour of your own input to an agent
ansi = ["#000000", "#ef4444", "#22c55e", "#eab308", "#3b82f6", "#a855f7", "#06b6d4", "#d1d5db",
        "#4b5563", "#f87171", "#4ade80", "#facc15", "#60a5fa", "#c084fc", "#22d3ee", "#ffffff"]

[effects]
scanline_opacity = 0.22  # the CRT page lists every key

[font]
family = "JetBrains Mono"  # optional, defaults to the desktop's monospace
size = 14                  # 8–32
cell_height = 20           # 10–48

Colours are six-digit hex, with or without #. bg, surface, text, accent, faint and all sixteen ansi entries are required. Left out, human is the accent's opposite hue, kept light. [effects] is described on the CRT page. A design with no font family uses the desktop's monospace, which Omarchy sets.

A second file named by TD_PALETTE, holding only [colors], is laid over custom, so an outside theme switcher can rewrite the colours without touching your effects. Named but missing is fine; present but broken keeps the current theme.

A design file can also say ui = "clean", the UI set it asks for, and carry a [look] table of the look dials it starts from, such as crt = true. A file that names no skin or set asks for ops and standard.4

Order of layers

The painted Omarchy theme, or the desktop's while INHERIT is on, replaces the design's colours first. Colours chosen with the colour sets and wheel of builds before 0.4.0 are still applied over it, though neither can be picked any more. Invert comes last. A pane that follows the window re-reads it on every frame; turning follow outer off and on again keeps whatever the pane had of its own.1

What would prove this wrong

Hide a theme in Omarchy's own picker. If its tile is still in the painter a second later, or a theme the painter offers is missing from Omarchy's list, the painter and Omarchy's picker disagree, and the THEME area exists to keep them the same.
